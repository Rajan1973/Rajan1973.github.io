What management said

Demand
  • Uniformly strong. Nobody flagged a demand problem.
  • Drivers named repeatedly: power, real estate, railways, solar, data centres.
  • Market sized at ₹1.2 trillion by end-FY27.
  • Organised share 68% to 73% since FY19 — the structural tailwind.
Margin guidance
  • RR Kabel holds 10.5% FY28 guidance despite nearly hitting 10% already. Deliberately conservative.
  • KEI margin expanded on mix — EHV up 47.7%, dealer share 51% to 59%.
  • Havells blamed A&P doubling to 4.4% of revenue, says it normalises over the year.
  • Finolex watching whether double-digit margins survive copper.
Capex — everyone is building
  • Polycab: ₹6,000–8,000cr over five years.
  • Havells: ₹1,400cr FY27, mostly cables.
  • RR Kabel Project Rise: ₹1,200cr FY26–28, ₹650cr in FY27.
  • KEI: ₹600–700cr annually; Sanand target raised to ₹7,000cr capacity.
  • Dynamic Cables commissioning Reengus greenfield.
Exports and tariffs
  • KEI expects exports back to 17–18% of revenue in FY27 as Middle East resumes.
  • RR Kabel is the sector's largest exporter — the most tariff-exposed.
  • Paramount says the US Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA punitive duties, restoring India's competitive position. Its exports rose ~77% sequentially.
  • Havells saw switchgear export disruption from West Asia.
  • That ruling matters most for RR Kabel, the largest exporter here, which has not been re-read against it.
On the entrants
  • Conspicuous silence. No incumbent management addressed it directly in Q1 commentary.
  • Nuvama's view: distribution must be built afresh for wires; approvals take 6–24 months.
  • Analyst consensus is that profitability stays stable initially. The word doing the work is "initially".
  • Adani's advantage is captive copper; UltraTech's is dealer overlap with cement.
Broker positioning
  • CLSA initiated: RR Kabel and Polycab Outperform, KEI Hold.
  • KEI's downgrade is on valuation, not execution — it had the best margin print.
  • Polycab holds 26–27% organised share, up from 18–19% in FY20.
  • RR Kabel trades near 56x trailing.

Red flags

Ranked by effect on position sizing, not by how alarming they sound.

Act on these
Havells
Cables incremental margin of 2.3%, segment margin down 218bps to 10.4% — and ₹1,400cr of capex going into that segment. PAT fell 16.6% while revenue grew 19.5%. Raw material cost rose 33.6%, well ahead of revenue.
Sector-wide
Every incumbent is adding capacity simultaneously, into a market two conglomerates are entering. Polycab ₹6,000–8,000cr, Havells ₹1,400cr, RR Kabel ₹1,200cr, KEI ₹600–700cr a year. That is a lot of supply arriving at once.
RR Kabel
56x trailing earnings after an 84% one-year move. Promoter holding has declined every quarter, down to 61.38% from 61.76%. Small, but consistently one-directional.
Monitor next quarter
KEI
Two different EBITDA margins are being reported: 13.04% standalone (+155bps) and 12.43% consolidated (+247bps). Both appear in circulation. The base years differ too. Reconcile against the filing before modelling.
Polycab
Revenue fell 7.4% sequentially. FMEG grew faster than wires and cables — good for mix, but the core segment is the one with the moat. EPC declined on timing.
Paramount
Three different EBITDA figures are circulating for the same quarter — ₹37.8cr, ₹34.7cr and ₹30.3cr, depending on treatment of other income. Reconcile against the filing before modelling. Finance costs rising, and the preferential issue at ₹42 dilutes well below the current market price.
Note and move on
Finolex
Stock jumped 12% on the print. Communication cables and agricultural wires were the pressure points a year ago; watch whether the fibre draw expansion lands into demand.
Dynamic Cables
Order book essentially flat sequentially at ₹811cr against ₹808cr. Revenue fell 1.8% QoQ. Growth is real but the book is not building.
Precision Wires
Operating margin fell to 4.78% from 5.34% while revenue grew 60%. That growth is copper price passing through a conversion business, not improving economics. The ₹150cr CCD issue converts at ₹400 within 12–18 months — dilution is scheduled, not hypothetical.

Sector synthesis

The market repriced this sector twice in 2025 on entrant fear — stocks fell up to 15% on the UltraTech announcement and again on Adani's. Scored against what Q1 FY27 actually shows.

Wrong
That entrant fear would show up in incumbent numbers by now. It has not. KEI expanded margin 247bps, RR Kabel 204bps, Finolex grew EBITDA 79%. No pure play shows pricing pressure.
Right
That the moat is distribution, not manufacturing. KEI's dealer-led sales rose 41.98% and now carry 59% of revenue against 51% a year ago. Its active dealer count is 2,128. That network is what an entrant has to replicate.
Partly
That the organised shift protects everyone. Organised share is up 68% to 73%, so entrants can grow without taking incumbent share. But that logic works until the market matures, and it says nothing about pricing.
Right
That the diversified players would look weaker. Havells fell 16.6% on PAT with cables incremental margin at 2.3%. The FMEG and A&P drag is real and the cables segment is not compensating.
Wrong
That Q1 tells you anything about the entrant threat. UltraTech's plant opens in December. The first quarter that contains any entrant volume is Q4 FY27 at the earliest, and meaningful volume a year after that. This report cannot answer the question the sector is actually being priced on.

The call

Own now

KEI Industries

Best margin expansion in the sector, and expansion of the right kind — mix-driven, from EHV up 47.7% and a dealer channel that went from 51% to 59% of sales. That dealer network is the specific asset an entrant cannot buy quickly. CLSA has it at Hold on valuation, which is a reason to size carefully, not to avoid it.

Right business, wrong price

RR Kabel · Polycab

RR Kabel had the sector's best quarter outright — revenue up 54%, PAT up 129%, FMEG at breakeven, debt/equity 0.1x, ROCE 25.8%. At 56x trailing it is priced for it. Polycab is the scale leader with 26–27% organised share. Both are quality; neither offers a margin of safety at these multiples. Buy on entrant-driven weakness, which the sector has produced twice before.

Stay out

Havells

The cables business grew 27% and earned 2.3% on the increment. Group PAT fell 16.6%. Management is directing ₹1,400cr of capex into the segment where its margin just fell 218bps, while A&P doubled to 4.4% of revenue. The market has already voted: −13.6% over one year, the only negative return in this universe, while RR Kabel and Apar more than doubled. The bull case is that A&P normalises — a promise about costs, not a change in unit economics.

What would make us more positive
  • A second consecutive quarter of margin expansion at the pure plays, confirming Q1 was mix and not copper timing.
  • KEI exports recovering to the guided 17–18% of revenue.
  • Any entrant-driven selloff of the kind seen in Feb and Mar 2025, when quality names fell 13–15% on announcement rather than on numbers.
What would make us cut
  • Evidence of discounting once Bharuch ships — watch realisation per unit, not revenue.
  • Incumbent dealer counts falling, or dealer share of revenue dropping. This is the leading indicator, ahead of margin.
  • A copper spike that the pass-through lag cannot absorb. Havells already shows raw material cost rising 33.6% against 19.5% revenue.
  • Capex plans being raised again across the board into an entrant wave.

Caveats

  • The central question cannot be answered from this quarter. UltraTech's plant is operational from December 2026 and Adani's venture is earlier stage. No entrant volume exists in any Q1 FY27 number. Everything in the synthesis about the entrant threat is inference from industry structure, not from the data.
  • Havells is not a cables company. Cables are ₹2,456cr of ₹6,518cr group revenue. Its group EBITDA margin of 7.15% is not a cable margin and is shown separately in the league table for that reason.
  • The Havells incremental margin is derived. Computed from reported segment revenue and EBIT against a prior-year base of roughly ₹1,934cr revenue and ₹243cr EBIT that was itself worked back from disclosed growth rates. The 2.3% figure is the report's sharpest claim and its least directly sourced. Verify against the segment filing before acting on it.
  • KEI's margin is reported two ways. 13.04% standalone against a 11.49% base, and 12.43% consolidated against a 9.96% base. This report uses the consolidated pair. The two are not reconcilable from public summaries.
  • All nine companies have now reported. Paramount and Precision Wires filed on 11 and 10 August respectively, after this report was first built. Their cards have been rewritten from the filings rather than left blank.
  • Precision Wires is still a different business. Winding wire for transformers and motors, sold on conversion margin. Its 4.78% operating margin is structurally lower than a building-wire margin for reasons that are not underperformance. It appears in the league table with that label attached; do not rank it against Polycab or KEI on the number alone.
  • Paramount's EBITDA is reported three ways. ₹37.8cr at a 7.1% margin from the earnings call, ₹34.74cr from one wire service, ₹30.31cr from another. This report uses operating profit excluding other income — ₹34.7cr at 6.6% — because that is what management framed the quarter around. The league table uses the 7.1% call figure and is flagged accordingly.
  • Apar was covered separately in the Power Transmission review. Its Q1 profit growth came mainly from speciality oils; conductor volumes fell on high metal prices. Reading its group numbers as a cables signal would be wrong.
  • Price and return data is exchange-sourced; fundamentals are not. Every figure in the returns table comes from Kite Connect on a single date. P/E and market cap are absent because that API carries neither — it has no EPS or shares-outstanding field. Do not infer valuation from this report.
  • Research depth is uneven on the earnings side. Polycab, KEI, Havells, RR Kabel, Paramount and Dynamic Cables are well covered. Finolex and Precision Wires are moderate — no Precision Wires earnings call transcript was located, so there is no management commentary for it in this report.
  • Precision Wires is the best performer in the table. Up 146.9% over a year, now shown with its Q1 numbers. The market is paying for the transformer and motor capex cycle, not for cable economics. Its 60% revenue growth on a falling operating margin is the same copper pass-through visible across the sector, only sharper because conversion margins are thinner.
  • Forward statements are projections. FY27 capex, margin and export targets are management guidance, not outcomes.
