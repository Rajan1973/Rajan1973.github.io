Large caps gave Friday back; small caps did not

The Nifty fell 0.70% and closed in the bottom third of its range after opening 230 points higher. Yet smallcaps rose 1.42%, more stocks advanced than declined, and the share of stocks above their 20-day average went up. The index had a bad day. The market underneath it did not.

What's Unusual Today

  • GALLANTT — 18% on 48 times normal volume
    Gallantt Ispat rose 18.23% on 47.59 times its typical 63-day volume, the largest volume anomaly in this report by a wide margin and the largest single-stock gain on the board. RSI at 64.4 is firm but not extreme, and the price now sits 11% above its 20-day average. The low ADX of 15.3 matters: that measures trend strength, and a reading under 20 means there was no established trend before today. This is a standing start, not an acceleration — the kind of move that comes from a specific event rather than a drift.
  • NIACL — 15% on 12 times volume, from a standing start
    New India Assurance rose 15.28% on 12.39x normal volume. Like Gallantt it carries a modest ADX of 22.7 and an unremarkable RSI of 56.5, and it now trades 6.1% above its 20-day average. Two of the day's three largest moves were insurers and metals-adjacent names with no prior trend, on a day the index fell. A move of this size in a government-owned general insurer is rarely technical.
  • PFOCUS — the media surge in one name
    Prime Focus rose 7.99% on 8.73x volume while still sitting 9.6% below its 20-day average, with RSI at 42.1. That combination — a large advance from a position well below trend — describes the entire Nifty Media move. The sector rose 4.60% today and five of its ten constituents reached the day's top-20 gainers. This is a sector being bought from a low base, not one extending a run.
  • TATACHEM — 8% with no trend behind it
    Tata Chemicals rose 8% on 5.61x volume with RSI at 49.9 — dead neutral — and an ADX of 17.3, below the threshold that signals any directional trend at all. It closed 2.2% under its 20-day average. A chemicals name moving 8% from a neutral technical position on a down day points to something specific to the company or its end-market pricing.
  • SYNGENE — a strong trend finally moving
    Syngene rose 7.07% on 6.17x volume, and it is the one name in this list with a genuinely strong trend behind it: ADX of 49.1, far above the 40 that marks a powerful directional move. RSI at 48.5 and a price sitting 0.4% from its 20-day average mean the move started from the middle, not from an extreme. It was also the sole healthcare name to rise meaningfully while Nifty Hospitals recorded an advance/decline ratio of zero.
  • CARTRADE — extended, and still rising
    CarTrade rose 8.14% on 4.36x volume with RSI at 62.8 and a price 8.2% above its 20-day average. Of the six stock anomalies here, this is the only one already extended before the move. Buying something 8% above its own short-term average on a day the index falls is a conviction trade, and it carries the corresponding risk if the conviction is wrong.
  • Nifty Financial Services — the largest breadth swing, with a lag 1 of 24
    Financial services breadth improved 30 percentage points over five sessions, ranking 1st of 24 indices. The caution is that this is a five-session measure and it is now describing the past: the sector fell 0.41% today, Nifty Bank recorded an advance/decline ratio of 0.08, and PSU Bank managed zero advances. The rotation that the rate hike set off last week is still in the five-day numbers, but it stopped working today.
  • Nifty 50 ATR — the range widened without result 165 of 250
    The Nifty's average true range — the typical gap between a day's high and low — rose to 1.13% of price from 1.09%, ranking 165th of 250 sessions. The index travelled 347 points between its high and low and finished 157 points down. More distance covered for a smaller net move is the signature of a market changing its mind intraday rather than trending.
  • Nifty 500 advance/decline — positive, on a down day 163 of 250
    271 advances against 227 declines gives a ratio of 1.19, ranking 163rd of the last 250 sessions. The ratio stayed above 1.0 while the index fell 0.70% — more stocks rose than fell on a losing day. That only happens when the decline is concentrated in the heaviest-weighted names, which is exactly what the index board shows.
  • Flows — the eighth consecutive session of foreign selling 23 of 60
    Foreign investors sold ₹1,708.5 crore, ranking 23rd heaviest of the last 60 sessions — a light day by recent standards, and far below Thursday's ₹12,943.6 crore. Domestic institutions bought ₹3,045.0 crore, absorbing 178% of it. The streak is now eight sessions without a single day of net foreign buying, and month-to-date the two sides stand at ₹41,487 crore sold against ₹43,400 crore bought.

Macro & Policy Dashboard

September CPI was due today 12 Oct · Scheduled

MoSPI release date · last confirmed reading is August at 4.82%
  • MoSPI scheduled the September 2026 Consumer Price Index for release on 12 October 2026, today.
  • We could not verify the September print at the time of writing, and are not reporting an unconfirmed figure. Searches returned several mutually contradictory numbers that mix different years and base series.
  • The last confirmed reading is August 2026 at 4.82%, up from 4.45% in July — rural 5.23%, urban 4.31%, food inflation 5.95%.
  • That series uses a 2024 base year and sits above the RBI's 4% medium-term target, which is the backdrop to last week's rate decision.
  • The official number is on mospi.gov.in. Treat any figure circulating before that page updates with suspicion.

The rate hike that set up this week 7 Oct · Hawkish

Repo 5.50% · calibrated tightening · next MPC 2-4 December
  • The RBI raised the repo rate 25 basis points to 5.50% on 7 October — the first increase in nearly four years — and moved its stance to calibrated tightening, explicitly removing near-term rate cuts.
  • It projected FY27 growth at 7.1% (raised 40bp) and CPI at 5.2%. A central bank forecasting inflation above its own target while raising growth is signalling it expects to stay tight.
  • The rationale named elevated crude, geopolitical tension, firm global bond yields and domestic inflation pressure.
  • Crude is not helping: the MCX October contract is up 1.15% as this is written, the only one of our five commodity contracts in the green.
  • There is no scheduled policy input before December. Between now and then the market trades on data, not decisions.

Searched, and not found Disclosure

The report date searched on its own, not just the calendar
  • We searched 12 October itself for unscheduled policy, currency intervention, reserves and bond-yield news and found no verifiable India-specific headline dated to the session.
  • We also searched for a cause behind the 4.60% Nifty Media surge and found none dated to this window. The move is real and is reported below; the explanation is not available.
  • US index closes, Brent, the dollar index and the US 10-year could not be verified for this date and are therefore absent from §5 rather than estimated.
  • IIP is due on the 28th. Dussehra closes the exchanges on the 20th.

A/D D-1 is the advance/decline for yesterday. %>20D D-1 is the percentage of stocks in that index trading above their 20-day SMA, yesterday.

A/D Today tells you how the index performed today — what the advance-to-decline ratio was. %>20D SMA tells you what percentage of stocks in that universe is trading above its 20-day simple moving average. Watch how the A/D ratio moved from last week to today, and how %>20D SMA moved over the same stretch — those two shaded pairs are the comparison this table exists for.

A rising index with increasing advances and more stocks above the 20D SMA means broader participation in the rally. A rising index on very narrow participation can instead mean index management, indecision, or only a few sectors doing the work while the rest of the market goes nowhere.

Twelve indices were flagged, and they divide into two mirror-image groups. The advancing side: Nifty Media (A/D 4.0) is the standout — 50% of its constituents now sit above their 20-day average, a 20-point gain in three sessions, and five of the day's 40 scanner names came from this 10-stock index, 6.25 times its fair share, all five of them gainers. Nifty Capital Goods (A/D 3.17, 30% above the 20-day line, +16 in three sessions) and Nifty Smallcap 100 (A/D 2.19, +9) show the same small-cap bid. Nifty Healthcare (+20 points in three sessions) improved on breadth despite an A/D of 0.9. Nifty Auto advanced 2:1 but still has zero constituents above their 20-day average — the second straight session in that state. The declining side is where Friday's leadership unwound: Nifty Bank (A/D 0.08), Nifty PSU Bank (A/D 0 — no advances at all), Nifty IT (0.25), Nifty Private Bank (0.25) and Nifty FMCG (0.25) all recorded near-total declines, after all five led Friday's rally. Nifty Hospitals is the sharpest: an A/D of zero, no constituent above its 20-day average, and five scanner names — 4.81 times its share, every one a decliner. Nifty Realty advanced 1.5:1 yet also shows zero constituents above their 20-day average. The pattern across all twelve: the rate-sensitive and large-cap groups gave back Friday, while media, capital goods and smallcaps absorbed the money.

Sector Rotation

Rotating in

  • Nifty Financial Services (+30 points of breadth over five sessions) — ranked 1st of 24, but this is a lagging window. The sector fell 0.41% today and its five-day gain is now describing a rotation that stalled.
  • Nifty Bank (+29) — same caveat, and sharper: an advance/decline ratio of 0.08 today, meaning roughly one advancing stock for every twelve that fell.
  • Nifty PSU Bank (+25) — fell 1.18% with zero advances. The rate-hike trade that drove banks last week did not survive into Monday.
  • Nifty Media (+20) — the only entry on this list that is still working. Up 4.60% today, 4.12% on the week, and first on one month (+5.70%) and three months (+9.47%).
  • Nifty Private Bank (+20) — 40% of constituents above their 20-day average, the joint-highest with IT, but an advance/decline ratio of 0.25 today.

Rotating out

  • Nifty Realty (−20 points) — the weakest rotation for a second straight session, down 3.53% on the week and 13.70% over three months, the worst on the board. Zero constituents above their 20-day average. Higher mortgage rates remain the most direct damage from the 7 October hike.
  • Nifty Oil & Gas (−7) — down 2.01% on the week with crude rising 1.15%. Refiners and marketers carry the input cost without pricing power.
  • Nifty FMCG (−7) — the day's worst sector at −1.67%, having been Friday's second-best at +2.20%. A one-day round trip, with an advance/decline ratio of 0.25.
  • Nifty Hospitals (0) — no breadth change because there was nothing left to lose: zero constituents above their 20-day average, and an advance/decline ratio of zero.
  • Nifty Auto (0) — advanced 2:1 and still has zero constituents above their 20-day average. Worst sector over one month at −8.13%.
  • Friday's rotation reversed almost exactly. The five groups that led the rally — IT, FMCG, Bank, Private Bank, PSU Bank — produced advance/decline ratios between 0.0 and 0.25 today. That is not profit-taking at the margin; it is the whole trade coming off.
  • The five-day rotation table is now misleading on its own. Financials still top it because of last week, while today they were among the weakest. Read §8 alongside today's breadth, never instead of it.
  • Media is the only sustained leadership on the board. First over one month and three months, up on the day, on the week, and gaining 20 points of 20-day participation in three sessions. No other sector is positive on both monthly horizons.
  • Zero-participation sectors are multiplying. Auto, Hospitals and Realty all show no constituent above its 20-day average. Friday had three such sectors too — and they are not the same three improving, they are the same three stuck.
  • What would change the read: financials regaining a positive advance/decline ratio within two sessions. If they do not, Friday was a squeeze inside a downtrend.

How to read this report

The order matters more than the sections. Most readers open at the index level and stop there — which is the habit that makes a narrow market look healthy.

1 Start with participation, not the index

Open at §7, not §1. For the broad indices — Nifty 50, Nifty 500, Midcap 100, Smallcap 100 — compare A/D today against A/D a week ago, then follow %>20D across today, D-1, D-2 and one week ago. You are looking for the direction of travel, not the level. Participation turns before price does.

2 Then check the index against it

This is the divergence test, and it is the most useful thing in the report. If the index is flat or mildly green while A/D sits below 0.70 and %>20D is falling, the index is masking selling underneath — a handful of heavyweights are carrying it. The reverse also counts: a red index on improving breadth is accumulation, not damage.

3 Read sector leadership across timeframes

In §6b, note which sectors are green and red on the day — then read the 1D against the 1W and 1M columns. A sector green today but red over a month is a bounce or a defensive hiding place. Green across all three is leadership. The difference decides whether a move is worth following.

4 Cross-check each sector's move against its own breadth

Take the green sectors from §6b and find their row in §7. Did the sector rise with A/D above 1.0 and a healthy %>20D, or did one or two heavyweights drag it up while most constituents fell? Watch the constituent count beside each index name: one stock is 10% of a ten-stock sector and 0.2% of the Nifty 500, so the same percentage means very different things.

5 Go straight to the flagged sectors

The report already joins breadth to the day's biggest movers, so you do not have to. Any sector called out in §7 or §8 tripped a threshold — participation shifting 20 points in three sessions, an A/D at 0.3 or 2.0, or its constituents filling far more of §9's top and bottom twenty than its size warrants. Those paragraphs are where the day's real story usually sits.

6 Finish by asking whether the money agrees with the positioning

Read §11 and §13 together. Institutions buying heavily in the cash market while open interest builds against a falling futures price is hedging, not conviction — and it explains a day when large buying fails to lift anything. When flows and positioning point the same way, the move has more behind it.
