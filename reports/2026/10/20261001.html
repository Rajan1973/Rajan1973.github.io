Resolves an earlier in-session discrepancy: a first search surfaced 55.7 (tradingeconomics.com) as the September reading, but a second, more targeted search surfaced 55.1 corroborated independently by three sources (Angel One, Tribune India, Business Standard) — two of which were fetched in full and matched each other closely down to an identical August baseline (52.8) and near-identical descriptive language. 55.1 is treated as the authoritative figure and 55.7 is superseded.

  • New orders: accelerated sharply, the fastest pace in several months, with firms specifically citing festive-season demand and the GST 2.0 rate cuts as direct tailwinds.
  • Output: production rose at its quickest rate since the survey's recent peak, firms running up inventory and capacity to meet the stronger order books.
  • Export orders: new export business also expanded, a positive signal given ongoing global demand uncertainty.
  • Employment: hiring intentions improved, with manufacturers adding headcount to support the stronger order pipeline — consistent with genuine capacity expansion rather than a one-off demand spike.
  • Input / output prices: input cost inflation picked up (partly a function of higher raw-material and crude-linked costs — note today's 3.14% crude jump in §1), but firms were broadly able to pass this through, keeping output-price inflation manageable.
  • Business confidence: optimism about the year-ahead outlook improved from August, with manufacturers citing the festive season and GST-driven demand recovery as the main supports.
The irony of the day: the macro data (PMI, two-wheeler sales) was genuinely strong, and the equity market sold off anyway — most sharply in exactly the sector (Auto) whose own data triggered the "sell the news" reaction. This is a useful case study in separating hard economic data from same-day market positioning: a 55.1 PMI print and double-digit YoY two-wheeler growth are unambiguously good news for Q3 GDP momentum, but NIFTY AUTO still fell 3.46% with a complete breadth washout. The regime score (§2) and the F&O positioning (§13) are both telling a cautious, derivatives-skeptical story even as the underlying economic data improves — a genuine divergence worth tracking into the next few sessions rather than reading today's equity weakness as a comment on the economy itself.
