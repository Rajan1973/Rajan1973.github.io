§1 · Executive Scorecard

▾

The order matters more than the sections. Most readers open at the index level and stop there — the habit that makes a narrow market look healthy.

1 · Start with participation, not the index

Open at §7, not §1. For the broad indices — Nifty 50, Nifty 500, Midcap 100, Smallcap 100 — compare A/D today against A/D a week ago, then follow %>20D across today, D-1, D-2 and one week ago. You are looking for the direction of travel, not the level.

2 · Then check the index against it

This is the divergence test. If the index is flat or mildly green while A/D sits below 0.70 and %>20D is falling, the index is masking selling underneath.

3 · Read sector leadership across timeframes

In §6b, note which sectors are green/red on the day, then read the 1D against the 1W and 1M columns. Green across all three is leadership; green today but red over a month is a bounce.

4 · Cross-check each sector's move against its own breadth

Take the green sectors from §6b and find their row in §7. Did the sector rise with A/D above 1.0, or did one or two heavyweights drag it up?

5 · Go straight to the flagged sectors

Any sector called out in §7 or §8 tripped a threshold — that's usually where the day's real story sits.

6 · Finish by asking whether the money agrees with the positioning

Read §11 and §13 together. Institutions buying heavily in cash while OI builds against a falling futures price is hedging, not conviction.
