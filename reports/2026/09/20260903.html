What's Unusual Today

  • Brigade Enterprises — 11.3% on 6.4x volume
    The day's most unusual single move. Brigade rose 11.3% on 6.4 times its three-month average volume, and now sits 13.7% above its 20-day average price with an RSI of 75.3. RSI above 70 is conventionally called overbought — it means the stock has risen fast enough that a pause would be normal. The ADX reading of 34.8 confirms a genuine trend rather than a one-day spike. This was part of a sector-wide realty rally, not a company-specific surprise.
  • Anant Raj — 6.7% on 8.8x volume
    The second realty name in the extremes, and the volume is the striking part: 8.8 times its three-month average. Unlike Brigade, Anant Raj is only 2.2% above its 20-day average and its RSI of 59.4 is neutral. That combination — huge volume, modest extension — is what an early move looks like rather than a late one.
  • Elecon Engineering — 4.64% on 11.5x volume
    The heaviest volume anomaly of the day: 11.5 times normal. Business Standard reported 65.92 lakh shares traded by mid-afternoon, roughly 21 times the two-week average. Yet the price only moved 4.64% and the stock sits 0.8% from its 20-day average with a neutral RSI of 49.9. Volume that large with price that still usually means shares changing hands in size between institutions, not a directional stampede. No company announcement was attached to it.
  • Foreign selling against domestic buying 7 of 38
    Foreigners sold ₹2,345.87 crore; domestic institutions bought ₹4,977.46 crore. Domestic buying covered 212% of the foreign selling. The foreign figure is the 7th largest sale in the 38 sessions of flow data we hold — large, but comfortably absorbed. Note this reverses yesterday, when foreigners bought ₹6,688 crore.
  • FirstCry — 4.6% on 8.3x volume, from a depressed base
    FirstCry rose 4.6% on 8.3 times normal volume, but the context is different from the others. Its RSI is 28.5 — below 30, conventionally oversold — and it trades 9.3% below its 20-day average. This is a bounce inside a decline, not a breakout. Heavy volume on such a bounce can mean buyers stepping in, or it can mean sellers finally finding a bid. One session does not tell you which.
  • Jyoti CNC — 7.45% on 2.6x volume
    The largest price move of the capital-goods names, and notably it did not need extreme volume — only 2.6 times average. The trend readings are the strongest in this list: ADX 38.2, RSI 66.8, and the stock is 10.6% above its 20-day average. A big move on ordinary volume in an established trend is usually continuation rather than a fresh event.
  • RBL Bank — 4.8% on 6x volume
    The only banking name in the anomaly list, up 4.8% on 6 times average volume, RSI 70.4 and 5.5% above its 20-day average. Its ADX of 17.8 is low, meaning there is no strong established trend yet — this is a sharp move out of a range rather than an acceleration of one. It sits inside the private-bank breadth surge described below.
  • Nifty 50 volatility — unusually quiet at 0.76% of price 62 of 250
    The index's average true range — how far it typically travels in a day — is 0.76% of its price. Only 62 of the last 250 sessions were calmer. Today's actual range was 151.95 points, about 0.64%. This matters because it sits alongside Brent up 7% in a week and an escalating Middle East conflict. Markets that stay this still while their inputs move that much tend not to stay still indefinitely.
  • Nifty 500 advance/decline at 1.51 177 of 246
    1.51 shares rose for every one that fell, stronger than 177 of the last 246 sessions. What makes it notable is the contrast: the headline index fell while the average share rose. Yesterday the same ratio was 0.49 — the reverse. Two consecutive sessions of the index and its constituents disagreeing points to money rotating between sectors rather than leaving the market.
  • Private bank breadth — 20-point swing in five sessions 1 of 24
    The share of Nifty Private Bank constituents trading above their 20-day average rose 20 percentage points over five sessions, to 50%. That is the largest such swing among all 24 indices we track. Today four private banks rose for every one that fell. Breadth improving faster than price is generally the healthier order of events — it means participation is broadening before the index confirms it.

Macro & Policy Dashboard

Services PMI — August 54.1

Released today · previous 53.3 · flash estimate 54.5
  • The HSBC India Services PMI, compiled by S&P Global, rose to 54.1 in August from 53.3 in July. Any reading above 50 means the sector is growing.
  • It came in below the 54.5 flash estimate published in late August, and fractionally below its own long-run average.
  • The weak spot is demand: new business grew at the second-slowest pace in over four years.
  • The bright spot is hiring — services firms added staff at the fastest rate in 15 months. Companies do not hire into a demand collapse, so this is worth more than the headline.

The rupee — a 10-week closing high 94.4850

Nothing else was scheduled today — this was the real domestic story
  • The rupee closed 0.5% stronger at 94.4850 per dollar, its best close since 25 June.
  • It did this while Brent crude rose above $96 — the opposite of the usual relationship, since costlier oil means India must buy more dollars to pay for it.
  • The reason is inflow, not oil: the RBI's special deposit schemes drew far heavier dollar inflows than economists had expected.
  • This is the level to watch. A rupee that holds under 95 despite $96 oil is doing something unusual, and it is the RBI's schemes doing the work.

Bond yields — the pressure point ~6.96%

India 10-year government bond · drifting higher
  • The 10-year government bond yield has been grinding higher, around 6.96% at the start of this week.
  • Two forces are pushing it: oil-driven inflation risk, and the RBI's August meeting minutes, which said policymakers could consider rate hikes if inflation risks broaden.
  • Higher yields make fixed deposits and bonds more competitive against shares, and they raise borrowing costs for companies.
  • Nothing else was on the domestic calendar in the last 48 hours. CPI inflation is due on the 12th; the next RBI policy meeting is 7 October.

Timing matters. US markets close after India's. The Dow, Nasdaq and S&P figures above are 2 September closes — they were a cue for today's Indian open, and tonight's US session is a cue for tomorrow. They never explain the session being reported. Brent, gold, silver and the rupee, by contrast, trade through Indian hours and did move this session.

The transmission chain, named. One asset move explains most of this report. Brent above $96, after Iranian missiles hit Kuwait, travelled into Indian equities along four separate paths. First, into the heavyweight index: refiners and marketers face a squeezed margin, which is why Nifty Oil and Gas fell 0.13% even though 2.75 of its constituents rose for every one that fell — the smaller upstream names gained while the weighted majors dragged. Second, into consumption: costlier crude means costlier packaging, transport and input costs, and FMCG closed with zero of 15 constituents above their 20-day average while auto shed 47 points of breadth. Third, into rates: oil-driven inflation risk pushed the Indian 10-year towards 6.96% and the US 10-year above 4.80%, which is why realty and private banks — the two most rate-sensitive sectors — rallied on the expectation that policy support continues, even as the bond market argued the opposite. Fourth, into safe havens: gold rose 2.17% and silver 2.53% on MCX, both purely geopolitical bids. The one path that did not conduct is metals: every MCX metal rose, yet Nifty Metal lost 27 points of breadth. Higher metal prices are not currently reaching metal shares.

A/D D-1 is the advance/decline for yesterday. %>20D D-1 is the percentage of stocks in that index trading above their 20-day SMA, yesterday.

A/D Today tells you how the index performed today — what the advance-to-decline ratio was. %>20D SMA tells you what percentage of stocks in that universe is trading above its 20-day simple moving average. Watch how the A/D ratio moved from last week to today, and how %>20D SMA moved over the same stretch — those two shaded pairs are the comparison this table exists for.

A rising index with increasing advances and more stocks above the 20D SMA means broader participation in the rally. A rising index on very narrow participation can instead mean index management, indecision, or only a few sectors doing the work while the rest of the market goes nowhere.

Ten sectors moved enough in breadth to flag, and they divide cleanly into a domestic-facing group that improved and an export- or consumption-facing group that deteriorated.

NIFTY CAPITAL GOODS — 3.17 constituents rose for every one that fell, up from 0.28 yesterday. It supplied 10 of the day's 40 scanner names from a 50-stock index, 2.5 times its fair share, and 8 of those 10 were gainers: Jyoti CNC, Finolex Cables, Apar Industries, Kirloskar Oil Engines, PTC Industries, Inox Wind, Voltamp and Welspun Corp. Against that, Kaynes and APL Apollo fell. The share above their 20-day average is still only 38% and has not moved in three sessions, so this was a broad up-day inside a sector that has not yet repaired itself.

NIFTY IT — the worst breadth in the market: 0.11, meaning roughly nine constituents fell for every one that rose, and the sector index dropped 0.85%. The share above the 20-day average has collapsed 50 points in three sessions, to 40%. The backdrop is well documented: Nifty IT is down about 29% in 2026 and roughly 39% from its December 2024 peak, on fears that AI tooling erodes the traditional services model. The oddity is that IT is still up 2.04% over one week, the best of any sector on that window — so what we are seeing is a sharp giveback of a recent bounce, not a fresh leg down.

NIFTY PVT BANK — four rose for every one that fell, and the share above the 20-day average jumped 20 points in three sessions to 50%, the largest breadth swing of any index we track. RBL Bank led (+4.8%); Federal Bank was the single decliner. The sector index rose 0.51%.

NIFTY REALTY — the day's best sector, up 2.58%, with 70% of constituents above their 20-day average after a 40-point jump in three sessions. Brigade rose 11.3% and Anant Raj 6.7%. Business Standard attributed the move to expectations of a revenue-growth recovery, strong luxury-housing demand, and policy support from lower interest rates, GST rationalisation and continued government focus on housing and infrastructure.

NIFTY HEALTHCARE — breadth fell, with only 25% of constituents above their 20-day average after a 20-point drop in three sessions, and roughly two fell for every one that rose. Torrent Pharma (−2.12%) was its only scanner name. The sector index fell 0.46%, and pharma fell the same. No sector-level news drove it; this looks like the unwinding of a crowded position — healthcare was the 7th-best sector over one month and has now given up 25 points of breadth.

NIFTY PSU BANK — three rose for every one that fell, with Bank of Maharashtra up 4.76%. The sector index rose 0.48% and it is the 2nd-best performer over one month. Breadth has improved 17 points in three sessions.

NIFTY OIL AND GAS — 2.75 rose for every one that fell and breadth improved 13 points in three sessions, yet the sector index fell 0.13%. That gap tells you the move was in the smaller constituents, not the heavyweights. This is the direct oil transmission: with Brent above $96, upstream producers gain on realisations while refiners and marketers face a squeezed margin, and the index is weighted towards the latter.

NIFTY MEDIA — 2.33 rose for every one that fell, from zero advances yesterday, and the index rose 1.74%. No constituent reached the day's extremes, so this was a broad, moderate recovery rather than a single name carrying the sector. Only 30% are above their 20-day average, so the damage is far from repaired.

NIFTY SMLCAP 100 — 2.33 rose for every one that fell and it supplied 17 scanner names, 2.13 times its share: nine gainers led by Brigade, Jyoti CNC and Anant Raj, against eight losers led by Deepak Fertilisers, Ola Electric and Pine Labs. The index rose 1.20%, the best of the board. But its five-day breadth change is negative 7 points, so today ran against its own recent direction.

NIFTY FMCG — the flag here is stark: not one constituent is above its 20-day average. Zero out of 15. The index fell 0.62% today, is down 8.03% over one month — the worst of any sector by a wide margin — and down 4.51% over three months. Godrej Consumer fell 2.76% on 4.9 times normal volume, and it is down roughly 26% over six months. A sector at 0% breadth is either near capitulation or in structural decline, and one session cannot distinguish between them.

Sector Rotation

Rotating in

  • NIFTY PVT BANK — breadth up 20 points over five sessions, the largest gain of any index. Now 6th over one month, so the price has yet to catch up with the participation.
  • NIFTY OIL AND GAS — up 13 points. Directly tied to Brent above $96, and one of only three sectors positive over one week (+0.94%).
  • NIFTY IT — up 10 points over five sessions and the best sector over one week at +2.04%, which sits oddly against today's near-total decline. A bounce being given back.
  • NIFTY REALTY — up 10 points, and already ranked 3rd over one month and up 20.14% over three months. Here breadth and price agree.
  • NIFTY PSU BANK — up 9 points, ranked 2nd over one month. Steady rather than dramatic.

Rotating out

  • NIFTY AUTO — breadth down 47 points over five sessions, by far the sharpest deterioration anywhere. Down 3.62% over one week and 4.56% over one month, and the sector index fell another 0.52% today.
  • NIFTY METAL — down 27 points, despite being the best sector over one month. A leader losing its participation is a warning worth more than a laggard losing it.
  • NIFTY HEALTHCARE — down 25 points, with only 25% of constituents above their 20-day average.
  • NIFTY NEXT 50 — down 22 points, and the worst board index over one week at −1.74%.
  • NIFTY PHARMA — down 20 points, having been 4th over one month. The same unwinding as healthcare.
Money is moving from what worked over the last month into what is domestically driven and rate-sensitive. Metal and pharma — the 1st and 4th ranked sectors over one month — are both shedding breadth fast, while private banks, PSU banks and realty are gaining it. That is a rotation towards the domestic economy and away from global-cycle and defensive plays. The complication is auto: it is falling on both measures, breadth and price, which is deterioration rather than rotation. And metal losing 27 points of breadth on a day when every MCX metal rose is the sharpest contradiction on the board — higher metal prices are not currently reaching metal shares.

Headline metrics

  • Max pain: 23,950 — 23,950 is the strike at which option writers as a group lose the least at expiry. It sits 77 points above today's close, so the mechanical pull is mildly upward into Tuesday's expiry — but max pain is a gravity estimate, not a forecast, and it moves as positions change.
  • Crossover strike: 23,900 — Put open interest exceeds call open interest at every strike up to and including 23,850. From 23,900 upward calls dominate. The structural read

The chain describes a market boxed between 23,600 and 24,000. The call wall sits at 24,000 with 18.55 million contracts of open interest — nearly three times the next-largest call strike and by far the heaviest position anywhere in the chain. That is where sellers have decided the index should not go, and it is only 127 points overhead. The put floor sits at 23,600 with 8.23 million contracts, 273 points below. Between them, the crossover at 23,900 is where spot closed. Meanwhile Nifty September futures show a textbook short buildup: price down 0.11% while open interest rose 3.19%, meaning new positions were opened on the short side rather than old longs being closed. The futures premium of 101 points over spot is normal carry, not a sentiment signal. Bank Nifty diverged — price up, open interest flat, which is mild short covering, and it is consistent with the private-bank breadth surge. How to read this report

The order matters more than the sections. Most readers open at the index level and stop there — which is the habit that makes a narrow market look healthy.

1 Start with participation, not the index

Open at §7, not §1. For the broad indices — Nifty 50, Nifty 500, Midcap 100, Smallcap 100 — compare A/D today against A/D a week ago, then follow %>20D across today, D-1, D-2 and one week ago. You are looking for the direction of travel, not the level. Participation turns before price does.

2 Then check the index against it

This is the divergence test, and it is the most useful thing in the report. If the index is flat or mildly green while A/D sits below 0.70 and %>20D is falling, the index is masking selling underneath — a handful of heavyweights are carrying it. The reverse also counts: a red index on improving breadth is accumulation, not damage.

3 Read sector leadership across timeframes

In §6b, note which sectors are green and red on the day — then read the 1D against the 1W and 1M columns. A sector green today but red over a month is a bounce or a defensive hiding place. Green across all three is leadership. The difference decides whether a move is worth following.

4 Cross-check each sector's move against its own breadth

Take the green sectors from §6b and find their row in §7. Did the sector rise with A/D above 1.0 and a healthy %>20D, or did one or two heavyweights drag it up while most constituents fell? Watch the constituent count beside each index name: one stock is 10% of a ten-stock sector and 0.2% of the Nifty 500, so the same percentage means very different things.

5 Go straight to the flagged sectors

The report already joins breadth to the day's biggest movers, so you do not have to. Any sector called out in §7 or §8 tripped a threshold — participation shifting 20 points in three sessions, an A/D at 0.3 or 2.0, or its constituents filling far more of §9's top and bottom twenty than its size warrants. Those paragraphs are where the day's real story usually sits.

6 Finish by asking whether the money agrees with the positioning

Read §11 and §13 together. Institutions buying heavily in the cash market while open interest builds against a falling futures price is hedging, not conviction — and it explains a day when large buying fails to lift anything. When flows and positioning point the same way, the move has more behind it.
