What's Unusual Today

  • IFCI — a 12.7% day on seven times normal volume
    The single most unusual move on the board. IFCI rose 12.67% on 7.5 times its three-month average volume, touching ₹99.45 — within a rupee of ₹100 for the first time in eighteen years. Its RSI is 77.6 and it now sits 20.4% above its own 20-day average, which is stretched by any measure. The trigger is in §10: SEBI signalling it is close to clearing the NSE listing.
  • The largest foreign buy in 37 sessions 37 of 37
    ₹6,688 crore of net foreign buying ranks first out of the 37 sessions we hold. It follows ₹7,986 crore of selling on 31 August. Two sessions, a ₹14,674 crore swing in direction — and, as §13 shows, it arrived alongside a third day of short buildup in index futures.
  • IT breadth swung 40 points in five days 1 of 24
    The widest five-session breadth move of any of the 24 indices we track. Forty percentage points more of the IT index is participating than a week ago — even though the sector fell 1.25% today. Participation is moving before price, which is the order in which leadership usually changes.
  • NIACL — the same catalyst, the second-largest move
    Up 7.18% on five times normal volume, with an RSI of 67.7 and an ADX of 35.1 — a reading above 30 means the trend has real strength behind it rather than being noise. It sits 8.1% above its 20-day average. This is the IFCI trade in a second name: both are state-linked financials with indirect exposure to an NSE listing, and both moved on the same day on the same news.
  • RRKABEL — the day's heaviest fall, and no news behind it
    Down 8.34% on 2.3 times normal volume, the worst single-stock move on the board. Its ADX of 43.8 is unusually high, meaning the move down is a strong directional trend rather than a wobble, yet its RSI of 42.6 is not yet oversold — there is room to fall further before it looks stretched. We found no dated company announcement. It belongs to the broader capital-goods de-rating: that group's breadth is down 28 points in five sessions.
  • Two stocks fell for every one that rose 56 of 246
    An advance-decline ratio of 0.49 across the Nifty 500 — weaker than on roughly three-quarters of the last 246 sessions. This is the number that matters most today, because it is the one that contradicts the flows. Institutions bought ₹9,501 crore and two-thirds of the market still went down.
  • AEGISVOPAK — a 6% move with no trend behind it
    Up 6% on four times normal volume, but with an ADX of just 14.3. A reading that low means there is no established trend at all — this was a single-day spike, not the continuation of a move. Its RSI of 58.9 is unremarkable and it sits only 4.8% above its 20-day average. Worth watching precisely because the volume arrived without a trend to explain it.
  • SAILIFE — momentum into an already-extended chart
    Up 4.54% on 5.2 times normal volume, with an RSI of 73.1. Above 70 is conventionally 'overbought' — not a sell signal, but it means the stock has already run hard before this move. It now sits 9% above its 20-day average, and its ADX of 32.8 confirms a genuine trend. One of the few healthcare names bid on a red day.
  • A calm tape underneath a nervous market 69 of 250
    The Nifty's average daily range is 0.76% of its price — narrower than on about three-quarters of the last 250 days. That is the striking thing about Wednesday: a war escalating, crude up nearly 5% and US yields at multi-year highs, and the index still moved in a 128-point band. India VIX at 11.59 says the same. Volatility has not arrived yet. It rarely stays away through this much news.
  • AIIL — selling into weakness, not a spike
    Down 5.91% on twice normal volume, with an RSI of 37.7 and a position 9.2% below its 20-day average. Unlike RRKABEL its ADX is a moderate 24.8, so this is a stock drifting lower rather than breaking down. It is the third financial-services name in this list, which is notable given IFCI and NIACL are in it for the opposite reason — the sector split hard in both directions on the same day.

Macro & Policy Dashboard

GST collections — ₹1.99 lakh crore in August NEW

Released Tue 1 Sep · +14.8% year on year
  • Gross collections of ₹1,99,853 crore, up 14.8% on a year earlier and just short of the ₹2 lakh crore mark.
  • The split matters more than the headline. Revenue from domestic transactions rose only 9.3% to ₹1.37 lakh crore. Revenue from imports rose 29% to ₹62,604 crore.
  • Refunds jumped 68% to ₹31,795 crore, which is why the net figure — ₹1.68 lakh crore — grew a much slower 8.3%.
  • Trader implication: a strong headline resting on imports rather than domestic demand. Read alongside crude at $95, a 29% jump in import-linked tax is not unambiguously good news for the trade balance.

Auto sales — a record August, and the sector fell anyway NEW

Released Tue 1 Sep · ~4.5 lakh passenger vehicles despatched
  • The best August on record for passenger vehicles. Maruti +35% to 1,76,971 units. Tata Motors +59% to 65,253, with EVs up 94%. Hyundai +23.6% to 54,396, its highest-ever August.
  • The four largest makers shipped about 3,55,900 vehicles, close to 79% of the market.
  • Nifty Auto was the worst sector on Wednesday, down 1.79%, with Hero MotoCorp −4.59% and Eicher −3.24%. Auto breadth has fallen 40 points in five sessions — the joint-worst of any index.
  • Trader implication: these are despatches to dealers, not sales to buyers, and they were loaded in ahead of the festive season. The market appears to be treating a record number as already priced, or as borrowed from the months ahead.

Manufacturing PMI eased to 52.9 NEW

Released Tue 1 Sep · 52.9 in August, from 53.5 in July
  • Still above 50, so factory activity is still expanding — the index is built so that 50 divides growth from contraction.
  • But the pace slowed for the month, from 53.5 to 52.9.
  • Trader implication: not weak enough to alarm, not strong enough to offset the oil and yield picture. It sits quietly on the negative side of the ledger.

US–Iran strikes resume — Brent through $95 CONTINUING

Escalated Tue 1 Sep – Wed 2 Sep · Brent $95.68
  • What changed since yesterday: a further round of US strikes took Brent from $91.28 to $95.68, roughly 4.8% in one session, and it is now the direct cause of Wednesday's gap-down open.
  • US shares fell on Tuesday on the same news, and the US 10-year Treasury yield reached 4.814% — its highest since November 2023.
  • India imports the overwhelming majority of its crude. Every dollar widens the trade deficit and presses the rupee, and it hits paints, tyres, aviation, chemicals and logistics on the margin.
  • Trader implication: this is an unscheduled variable with no release date to position around. Oil has now outweighed a 7.8% GDP print, a record GST figure and a record auto month, three sessions running.

Sector Rotation

Rotating in

  • Nifty IT — breadth up 40 points in five sessions, the widest swing on the board, and the sector is up 2.59% over the week even after Wednesday's 1.25% fall. It ranks third over one month. Participation is broadening before the price has followed.
  • Nifty Private Bank — breadth up 10 points, fifth over one month. Quiet, steady improvement rather than a surge.
  • Nifty Pharma — breadth up 5 points, fourth over one month, and up 0.58% on the week. The defensive bid is intact.

Rotating out

  • Nifty Metal — breadth down 40 points, the joint-worst on the board, despite ranking first over one month at +3.45%. The leader is losing its followers.
  • Nifty Auto — breadth down 40 points, and the worst sector on the day at −1.79%. A record August despatch number did not stop it.
  • Nifty PSU Bank — breadth down 33 points while ranking second over one month. The same pattern as Metal.
  • Nifty Media — breadth down 30 points, down 4.77% over the week, thirteenth of fourteen over one month. Weak and getting weaker.
The two strongest sectors of the past month — Metal and PSU Bank — have the two worst breadth swings of the past week. That is what the end of a leadership run tends to look like: the price holds up while the number of participating stocks quietly drains away. Money appears to be moving toward IT and Pharma, which are gaining participation before they gain price. Watch whether Metal's price finally follows its breadth down, or whether breadth recovers to meet the price.

Headline metrics

  • PCR (OI): 0.900 — For every 100 call contracts outstanding there are 90 put contracts. Below 1.0 leans mildly bearish — more traders have sold upside than downside.
  • Max pain: 23,900 — The level at which option writers lose the least if the market settled today. Spot closed 14 points above it — about as pinned as it gets.
  • Crossover strike: 23,900 — Above 23,900 calls outnumber puts; below it, puts outnumber calls. That makes it the hinge of the current structure, and price is sitting on it.

The structural read

The chain describes a market boxed between 23,800 and 24,200. The heaviest call position sits at 24,200 with 10.1 million contracts — sellers there are betting the index does not reach it by Tuesday. The heaviest put position sits at 23,800 with 7.9 million, with a second, deeper base at 23,600 holding 6.8 million. Between those two walls, max pain sits precisely at the money.

The more interesting signal is in futures. September open interest rose 511,420 contracts while price fell 90 points — new shorts, not closing longs. That is now a third consecutive session of the same pattern: since 28 August, open interest has climbed from 15.37 million to 16.56 million, an increase of 1.19 million contracts, while the futures price fell 341 points. Somebody has been building a short position steadily for a week.

Set that against §11: foreigners bought ₹6,688 crore of cash equities the same day. The natural reading is hedged rather than directional — long the shares, short the index — which is exactly the combination that produces heavy buying and a falling market at the same time.

How to read this report

The order matters more than the sections. Most readers open at the index level and stop there — which is the habit that makes a narrow market look healthy.

1 Start with participation, not the index

Open at §7, not §1. For the broad indices — Nifty 50, Nifty 500, Midcap 100, Smallcap 100 — compare A/D today against A/D a week ago, then follow %>20D across today, D-1, D-2 and one week ago. You are looking for the direction of travel, not the level. Participation turns before price does.

2 Then check the index against it

This is the divergence test, and it is the most useful thing in the report. If the index is flat or mildly green while A/D sits below 0.70 and %>20D is falling, the index is masking selling underneath — a handful of heavyweights are carrying it. The reverse also counts: a red index on improving breadth is accumulation, not damage.

3 Read sector leadership across timeframes

In §6b, note which sectors are green and red on the day — then read the 1D against the 1W and 1M columns. A sector green today but red over a month is a bounce or a defensive hiding place. Green across all three is leadership. The difference decides whether a move is worth following.

4 Cross-check each sector's move against its own breadth

Take the green sectors from §6b and find their row in §7. Did the sector rise with A/D above 1.0 and a healthy %>20D, or did one or two heavyweights drag it up while most constituents fell? Watch the constituent count beside each index name: one stock is 10% of a ten-stock sector and 0.2% of the Nifty 500, so the same percentage means very different things.

5 Go straight to the flagged sectors

The report already joins breadth to the day's biggest movers, so you do not have to. Any sector called out in §7 or §8 tripped a threshold — participation shifting 20 points in three sessions, an A/D at 0.3 or 2.0, or its constituents filling far more of §9's top and bottom twenty than its size warrants. Those paragraphs are where the day's real story usually sits.

6 Finish by asking whether the money agrees with the positioning

Read §11 and §13 together. Institutions buying heavily in the cash market while open interest builds against a falling futures price is hedging, not conviction — and it explains a day when large buying fails to lift anything. When flows and positioning point the same way, the move has more behind it.
