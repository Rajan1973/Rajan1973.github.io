What's Unusual Today

  • New India Assurance — 17.7% on 7.6x volume
    The most unusual move in the market by a distance, and the pack scores it a perfect 100. NIACL rose 17.7% on 7.6 times its three-month average volume, hitting a 52-week high of ₹229.30, and now trades 22.6% above its 20-day average with an RSI of 78.7. RSI above 70 is conventionally called overbought — the stock has risen far enough, fast enough, that a pause would be normal. Its ADX of 39.6 is the strongest trend reading in the market today. The trigger is real and dated: anticipation of the National Stock Exchange's IPO, in which NIACL holds a stake.
  • Domestic buying at a 39-session record 5 of 39
    Foreigners sold ₹3,112 crore; domestic institutions bought ₹8,930 crore. That domestic figure is the largest of every session in our 39-day window — not merely large, the largest. Absorption was 287%, meaning domestic money covered the foreign exit almost three times over. The foreign sale was itself the 5th largest of the 39. Both sides pressed hard, and the domestic side won.
  • HBL Engineering — 4.54% on 12.3x volume
    The heaviest volume reading of the day: 12.3 times its three-month average, for a price move of only 4.54%. The stock sits 2.1% from its 20-day average with a neutral RSI of 51.1. That pattern — enormous volume, ordinary price move, no extension — usually means large blocks changing hands between institutions rather than a directional rush. No company announcement was attached.
  • KEI Industries — down 8.95% on 5.5x volume
    The day's worst faller and the clearest single-name casualty of the UltraTech news. KEI dropped 8.95% on 5.5 times normal volume and now sits 12.8% below its 20-day average with an RSI of 34 — approaching the 30 level conventionally called oversold. Its ADX of 23.9 says a downtrend is forming but not yet entrenched. This is what a de-rating looks like on day one: the market is not marking down this year's earnings, it is marking down the multiple it will pay for future ones.
  • Supreme Petrochem — 6.23% on 9.2x volume
    Up 6.23% on 9.2 times average volume, sitting 8.3% above its 20-day average with an RSI of 67. The oddity is the ADX of 14.3 — very low, meaning there is no established trend at all. A large move on huge volume with no trend behind it is a fresh event rather than a continuation, but no dated company news was found to explain it.
  • Tejas Networks — 8.23% on 6x volume
    Up 8.23% on 6 times average volume, now 15.4% above its 20-day average with an RSI of 72.3 — overbought territory. The company has been publicising large network wins, including router supply for BharatNet Phase III and international traction for its optical products, but nothing was dated specifically to this session.
  • Affle — 6.4% on 8.4x volume, against its sector
    Up 6.4% on 8.4 times normal volume — and notable because it is classified under Information Technology, the sector where roughly four constituents fell for every one that rose today. Affle still trades 2.1% below its 20-day average with a neutral RSI of 49.7, so this is a recovery from a weak base, not a breakout. A stock moving hard against its own sector is worth watching for a second session before drawing conclusions.
  • Nifty 50 volatility — quieter still, at 0.74% of price 45 of 250
    The index's average true range — how far it typically travels in a day — fell to 0.74% of price. Only 45 of the last 250 sessions were calmer, down from 62 yesterday. Today's actual range was just 109.9 points, about 0.46%. The market is getting quieter by the day while the Strait of Hormuz remains disrupted. That combination is the single most uncomfortable fact in this report.
  • Oil & Gas breadth — a 27-point swing in five sessions 1 of 24
    The share of Nifty Oil and Gas constituents above their 20-day average rose 27 percentage points over five sessions, the largest such move among all 24 indices we track. The sector is also the best board or sector performer over one week, up 1.08%. With Brent near $95 and Hormuz traffic reduced, money has been moving into energy names steadily rather than in one jump.
  • Nifty 500 advance/decline at 0.88 116 of 246
    0.88 shares rose for every one that fell — almost exactly the middle of the historical range, ranking 116th of the last 246 sessions. The pack scores this the least unusual item on the list, and that is the point of including it: on a day with a 17% mover, a record domestic buy and an industry-wide de-rating, the overall breadth reading was utterly ordinary. The drama was concentrated, not general.

Macro & Policy Dashboard

Forex reserves — an all-time high $740.8bn

Released today · week ended 28 August · up $11.475bn
  • India's foreign exchange reserves rose $11.475 billion in a single week to a record $740.803 billion. This is the RBI's regular Friday release.
  • Foreign currency assets, the largest component, rose $9.337 billion to $600.67 billion. Gold holdings rose $2.191 billion to $116.409 billion.
  • The RBI says the banking system has now mobilised $136.38 billion through its special dollar-rupee swap facility.
  • Reserves are the country's buffer against a falling currency and an oil bill paid in dollars. At a record high, with crude near $95, that buffer is doing exactly the job it exists for.

The rupee — holding its gains 94.4310

Near a two-month high · a second day of strength
  • The rupee firmed again to around 94.43 per dollar, close to its strongest in more than two months.
  • This is the second consecutive session of a stronger rupee while oil sits near $95 — the opposite of the textbook relationship.
  • The explanation is the reserves number above: dollar inflows through the RBI's swap facility are outrunning the oil bill.
  • Watch 95.00. The rupee has now put clear distance between itself and that level.

Bond yields — still the pressure point ~6.94%

India 10-year government bond · little changed
  • The 10-year government bond yield sat around 6.94%, broadly where it was mid-week.
  • Investors are weighing rebounding crude against doubts over the US-Iran ceasefire, with inflation risk on both sides of the ledger.
  • The RBI's August minutes flagged that policymakers could consider rate hikes if inflation risks broaden — which is why this number matters more than usual.
  • Nothing else was on the domestic calendar. CPI inflation lands on the 12th; the next RBI policy meeting is 7 October.

Timing matters. US markets close after India's. The Dow, Nasdaq and S&P figures above are 3 September closes — they were the cue for today's Indian open, and tonight's US session is the cue for Monday. They never explain the session being reported. Brent, gold, silver and the rupee trade through Indian hours and did move today.

The transmission chain, named. Today reverses yesterday's chain almost exactly. Yesterday oil rose and fear rose with it; today one Federal Reserve official said inflation looked to be improving, US rate-hike odds fell, Wall Street had its best day in a month, and the effect travelled into India along three paths. First, into the open: the Sensex gapped up and snapped a four-day losing streak, and Nifty September futures moved from a short buildup to short covering. Second, into the currency: softer US rate expectations weakened the dollar, emerging-market currencies firmed, and the rupee held near a two-month high — which, combined with record reserves, is why India could absorb $95 oil without strain. Third, into safe havens, in reverse: gold fell 1.54% and silver 1.60% on MCX, unwinding yesterday's geopolitical bid. Where the chain did not reach is the domestic economy — 264 shares still fell, FMCG still has zero constituents above their 20-day average, and Auto breadth is down 46 points in five sessions. A friendlier global backdrop lifted the index; it did not fix what is wrong beneath it. And one purely domestic event, UltraTech's entry into wires and cables, overwhelmed all of it for an entire industry.

A/D D-1 is the advance/decline for yesterday. %>20D D-1 is the percentage of stocks in that index trading above their 20-day SMA, yesterday.

A/D Today tells you how the index performed today — what the advance-to-decline ratio was. %>20D SMA tells you what percentage of stocks in that universe is trading above its 20-day simple moving average. Watch how the A/D ratio moved from last week to today, and how %>20D SMA moved over the same stretch — those two shaded pairs are the comparison this table exists for.

A rising index with increasing advances and more stocks above the 20D SMA means broader participation in the rally. A rising index on very narrow participation can instead mean index management, indecision, or only a few sectors doing the work while the rest of the market goes nowhere.

Eight sectors moved enough in breadth to flag. They split into a banking-and-energy group that is quietly strengthening and a manufacturing-and-defensive group that is deteriorating fast.

NIFTY IT — the worst breadth for a second consecutive day: 0.25, meaning four constituents fell for every one that rose, and the share above the 20-day average has now dropped 40 points in three sessions to 30%. The sector index fell 0.47% and is down 1.88% over the week. This is the same structural story — Nifty IT is down roughly 29% in 2026 on fears that AI tooling erodes the traditional services model — and the bounce we flagged yesterday has now been fully surrendered.

NIFTY HEALTHCARE — breadth of 0.18, worse than IT: roughly five constituents fell for every one that rose. The share above the 20-day average is 25% after a 20-point drop in three sessions, and the sector index fell 0.87%, the worst of any sector today. Over one week it is down 2.32%. Pharma fell 0.68% alongside it and has lost 15 points of breadth. Two sectors that were 7th and 4th ranked a month ago are now unwinding together, with no news attached — the signature of a crowded position being exited.

NIFTY PSU BANK — the share above the 20-day average rose 25 points in three sessions to 50%, one of the largest improvements on the board. But note the tension: today's advance/decline was only 0.83, and the index fell 0.44%. Breadth improving over three sessions while price falls today is a normal, healthy pause, not a reversal.

NIFTY CAPITAL GOODS — the day's most dramatic reversal. Yesterday 3.17 constituents rose for every one that fell; today 0.61. It still supplied 9 of the 40 scanner names from a 50-stock index, 2.25 times its fair share — but where yesterday 8 of 10 were gainers, today 6 of 9 are losers: KEI, Polycab, Finolex Cables, Kirloskar Oil Engines, Thermax and Jyoti CNC, against gains in Carborundum Universal, HBL Engineering and APL Apollo. Finolex Cables and Jyoti CNC were gainers in this same table yesterday. The UltraTech announcement is doing most of this work.

NIFTY REALTY — a sharp giveback. Yesterday's best sector, up 2.58%, fell 0.90% today with an advance/decline of 0.25 — four constituents down for every one up. Brigade, yesterday's 11.3% star, dropped 2.27% on above-average volume. The five-day breadth trend is still positive at +10 points and the sector remains 2nd over one month and up 18.74% over three, so this reads as profit-taking after a vertical day rather than a change of direction.

NIFTY AUTO — the most damaged sector in the market. An advance/decline of 0.15 means roughly seven constituents fell for every one that rose. Just 7% are above their 20-day average — one stock in fifteen. Breadth is down 46 points over five sessions, by far the worst on the board, and the index has fallen 3.95% in a week and 4.58% in a month. This is now deterioration on every measure at once.

NIFTY METAL — the one clear bright spot. 2.75 constituents rose for every one that fell, the index gained 1.07% — the best sector of the day — and 53% now sit above their 20-day average. This resolves the contradiction we flagged yesterday, when every MCX metal rose but metal shares lost 27 points of breadth. Today the shares followed, even though the metals themselves fell. APL Apollo was the sector's scanner name.

NIFTY FMCG — a second consecutive day with zero of fifteen constituents above their 20-day average. The advance/decline was 0.36 and the index fell 0.14%, and it remains the worst sector over one month at −7.34% and over three months at −4.82%. Two days at absolute zero breadth is no longer a single bad session; it is a sector where not one constituent can hold a short-term average.

The rotation has sharpened into something simpler than yesterday: money is moving into banks and energy, and out of manufacturing and defensives. All three banking indices and oil and gas are in the top five for breadth gains; auto, consumer durables, healthcare and IT are all in the bottom five. That is a bet on domestic credit growth and on energy prices staying elevated, funded by selling the sectors most exposed to consumer demand and to global technology spending. Two cautions. First, Nifty Next 50 losing 26 points of breadth is not a sector call — it is a size call, and it says the selling is broad among mid-large names. Second, capital goods flipped from +14 to −12 on the five-day measure in a single session because of one announcement, which is a reminder that these readings can be dominated by a single event rather than a trend.

How to read this report

The order matters more than the sections. Most readers open at the index level and stop there — which is the habit that makes a narrow market look healthy.

1 Start with participation, not the index

Open at §7, not §1. For the broad indices — Nifty 50, Nifty 500, Midcap 100, Smallcap 100 — compare A/D today against A/D a week ago, then follow %>20D across today, D-1, D-2 and one week ago. You are looking for the direction of travel, not the level. Participation turns before price does.

2 Then check the index against it

This is the divergence test, and it is the most useful thing in the report. If the index is flat or mildly green while A/D sits below 0.70 and %>20D is falling, the index is masking selling underneath — a handful of heavyweights are carrying it. The reverse also counts: a red index on improving breadth is accumulation, not damage.

3 Read sector leadership across timeframes

In §6b, note which sectors are green and red on the day — then read the 1D against the 1W and 1M columns. A sector green today but red over a month is a bounce or a defensive hiding place. Green across all three is leadership. The difference decides whether a move is worth following.

4 Cross-check each sector's move against its own breadth

Take the green sectors from §6b and find their row in §7. Did the sector rise with A/D above 1.0 and a healthy %>20D, or did one or two heavyweights drag it up while most constituents fell? Watch the constituent count beside each index name: one stock is 10% of a ten-stock sector and 0.2% of the Nifty 500, so the same percentage means very different things.

5 Go straight to the flagged sectors

The report already joins breadth to the day's biggest movers, so you do not have to. Any sector called out in §7 or §8 tripped a threshold — participation shifting 20 points in three sessions, an A/D at 0.3 or 2.0, or its constituents filling far more of §9's top and bottom twenty than its size warrants. Those paragraphs are where the day's real story usually sits.

6 Finish by asking whether the money agrees with the positioning

Read §11 and §13 together. Institutions buying heavily in the cash market while open interest builds against a falling futures price is hedging, not conviction — and it explains a day when large buying fails to lift anything. When flows and positioning point the same way, the move has more behind it.
