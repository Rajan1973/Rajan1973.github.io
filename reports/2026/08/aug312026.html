Macro & Policy Dashboard

Q1 FY27 GDP BIG BEAT

Released Mon 31 Aug 2026 · MoSPI
  • Real GDP grew 7.8%, against economist estimates of 7.1–7.3% and the RBI's 7.0% projection.
  • Real GDP ₹81.36 lakh crore, up from ₹75.46 lakh crore a year earlier. Nominal growth topped 10.3%.
  • Gross value added rose 8.2% — broader than the headline, which is the healthier detail.
  • Trader implication: a beat this size would normally lift the market. It did not, and that gap is the day's real story — see §12.

US strikes near Strait of Hormuz OIL SHOCK

Sunday 30 Aug 2026 · geopolitical
  • US forces struck Iranian rocket launchers near the Strait of Hormuz, through which a large share of seaborne crude passes.
  • Brent rose 2.93% to $90.69, having been $88.29 at Friday's close.
  • India imports the overwhelming majority of its crude. Higher oil widens the trade deficit, pressures the rupee, and squeezes margins for paints, tyres, aviation and chemicals.
  • Trader implication: this is the counterweight that cancelled the GDP beat. Watch oil before watching anything domestic tomorrow.

MSCI index rebalance MECHANICAL

Effective after close, 31 Aug 2026
  • A scheduled MSCI reshuffle forced index funds to trade in the closing auction, where liquidity is thin and prices move fast.
  • The Adani group lost ₹1.4 lakh crore — about $15 billion — its worst day in roughly 21 months.
  • Adani Energy Solutions was added to the MSCI Global Standard index, with roughly $310 million of expected inflows, and still fell over 10%.
  • Trader implication: treat today's Adani prints as a mechanical event. Rebalance distortions usually unwind within a few sessions; they are not a verdict on the businesses.

A/D D-1 is the advance/decline for yesterday. %>20D D-1 is the percentage of stocks in that index trading above their 20-day SMA, yesterday.

A/D Today tells you how the index performed today — what the advance-to-decline ratio was. %>20D SMA tells you what percentage of stocks in that universe is trading above its 20-day simple moving average. Watch how the A/D ratio moved from last week to today, and how %>20D SMA moved over the same stretch — those two shaded pairs are the comparison this table exists for.

A rising index with increasing advances and more stocks above the 20D SMA means broader participation in the rally. A rising index on very narrow participation can instead mean index management, indecision, or only a few sectors doing the work while the rest of the market goes nowhere.

The stand-out is that the percentage of Nifty 500 stocks above their 20-day average did not move at all — 41% on Friday, 41% today. The advance/decline ratio collapsed from 0.83 to 0.56, but the underlying trend positions did not deteriorate. That combination says today was concentrated damage in a few heavy names, not a broad breakdown.

§8

Sector Rotation

Rotating in

  • Hospitals — participation jumped 46 points in five sessions, the largest rotation on the board. Nothing else came close.
  • Information Technology — up 40 points of participation over the week and 1.94% on the week, holding the gains from Friday's Nvidia-driven move.
  • Healthcare and Pharma — up 35 and 25 points respectively, both up more than 3% on the week. Together with hospitals, this is a clear defensive bid.
  • Banks — participation up 21 points, and the sector rose 0.92% today while the index fell. Private banks led at +0.97%.

Rotating out

  • Realty — participation down 30 points in five sessions, the worst on the board, and down 1.41% on the week.
  • Media — also down 30 points, and the worst sector today at −2.84%. Down 3.79% over the month with no sign of a base.
  • Auto — down 26 points of participation despite being roughly flat on the day. Deteriorating quietly.
  • Metal — down 20 points, and the day's worst large sector at −2.45%. It remains the month's best performer at +3.73%, which makes this a reversal of the leader.
  • PSU Banks — down 17 points even though the sector closed marginally positive. Private banks are being preferred over public ones.
This is a defensive rotation, and it is unusually clean. Money moved into hospitals, pharma, healthcare and private banks; it moved out of realty, media, auto and metals. That is the shape a market takes when it is worried about growth and input costs — which is a strange reaction to a 7.8% GDP print, and tells you the oil price mattered more today than the growth number.
