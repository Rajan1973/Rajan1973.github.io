4c · Flow interpretation

The tug-of-war intensified sharply on Friday without changing its direction. FIIs have been near-flat across August as a whole (+₹454.73 Cr over 20 sessions), so the ₹5,039.80 Cr sale is an outlier rather than the continuation of a trend — it lines up with the dollar index jumping 0.52% and US yields rising on Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks, which reads as a macro-driven reallocation rather than a verdict on Indian fundamentals. DII absorption is the more important structural fact: ₹53,679 Cr bought month to date, and Friday's +₹5,183.93 Cr covered 103% of the foreign sale. That absorption is what has held the index up while breadth decayed. The risk it creates is that domestic buying is supporting price without supporting participation — and if the flows reverse, there is less underneath than the index level suggests.

6d · Structural shift analysis

7d · The critical observation

The two index futures books disagree with each other, and one of them disagrees with the cash market. Nifty September futures added 359,840 contracts on a rising price — fresh longs — on the same session that FIIs dumped ₹5,039.80 Cr of cash equity, the largest outflow in the retained window. BankNifty futures did the opposite, adding open interest on a falling price: fresh shorts. So on one day the market added index longs, added bank shorts, and sold cash in size. The reading that fits all three is that the cash selling is foreign and macro-driven — a dollar and yield story, per §2 — while the futures longs are domestic and tactical, positioned for the GDP print, with the bank shorts as the hedge against the very sector whose breadth collapsed from 64.3% to 21.4% above the 20-day SMA this week. That is a coherent book, but it is a leveraged one: it needs Monday's number to cooperate. Friday's long buildup was also the weakest of the week at +359,840 against +775,645 the day before, so conviction was already fading as the print approached.
