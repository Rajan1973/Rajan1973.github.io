Sector rotation (RRG) — FAQ

What is a Relative Rotation Graph (RRG)?

A Relative Rotation Graph plots each NSE sector against a benchmark (the Nifty 500) on two axes — RS-Ratio, which measures relative strength, and RS-Momentum, which measures whether that strength is rising or fading. It shows at a glance which sectors are leading, weakening, lagging or improving. It describes market structure for research and education only — not investment advice.

What do the four quadrants mean?

Leading (top-right) = outperforming the market and still gaining momentum. Weakening (bottom-right) = still strong but momentum fading. Lagging (bottom-left) = underperforming and still falling. Improving (top-left) = still weak but momentum turning up. Sectors typically rotate clockwise: Improving to Leading to Weakening to Lagging.

How are RS-Ratio and RS-Momentum calculated?

RS-Ratio normalises a sector's price relative to the Nifty 500 around a baseline of 100. RS-Momentum is the normalised rate of change of that relative-strength line, so it leads the ratio. Both use a weekly timeframe and refresh after each daily NSE scan.

Which sectors and benchmark does it use?

The graph covers the NSE sectoral indices — such as IT, Financials, Auto, Pharma, FMCG, Metal, Energy and Realty — measured against the Nifty 500. You can also switch to the broad-market indices view.

Is the sector rotation graph a buy or sell signal?

No. It is a descriptive, educational view of how sectors are rotating relative to the market. It is not investment advice and not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security.
