Why this is split

The eleven names here run on different cycles and cannot share a league table. Ranking them together would produce a tidy chart that means nothing.

IT services — 4 names
  • Persistent, Coforge, Zensar, Happiest Minds.
  • Driven by BFSI and enterprise discretionary spend.
  • Judged on EBIT margin, constant-currency growth, TCV.
ER&D — 5 names
  • LTTS, KPIT, Tata Elxsi, Tata Technologies, Cyient.
  • Driven by automotive and aerospace R&D budgets, not IT budgets.
  • European OEM spending cuts are this quarter's single cause.
CPaaS — 2 names
  • Tanla, Route Mobile. Added at request; not in the standard universe.
  • Driven by A2P messaging volume and telco termination rates.
  • Core metric is gross margin, not EBIT margin. Framework derived, not standard.

What management said

Demand — services
  • Persistent: 25th consecutive quarter of growth, record TCV.
  • Coforge claims it will be industry growth leader for a third year.
  • Happiest Minds reaffirmed 12.5% FY27 growth, pipeline up 20% QoQ.
  • Nobody in services described a demand problem.
Demand — ER&D
  • KPIT named the cause precisely: two large customers, one German, one Japanese, cutting engineering spend.
  • Its 23-quarter growth streak ended.
  • Cyient's mid-single-digit FY27 target now looks difficult after a soft Q1.
  • Tata Technologies is the dissenter, guiding double-digit organic growth for FY27.
Margin guidance
  • Coforge FY27: 15.5%+ consolidated EBIT, 16.5–17% standalone. Q1 already at 16.0%.
  • KPIT FY27 EBITDA 20.5–21.2%; Q1 came in at 16.2%.
  • Tata Elxsi targets EBITDA back to 27–28% with utilisation toward 80%. Q1 was 21.2%.
  • Cyient pushed its 15% EBIT target from Q4 FY27 to H1 FY28.
The AI claim
  • Coforge attributes margin expansion to AI in delivery; 86% of revenue is AI-led engineering, data and cloud.
  • KPIT expects AI-led productivity to lift margins each quarter from here.
  • Happiest Minds calls AI the largest value-creation opportunity in the industry's history.
  • Every company claims AI benefit. Only Coforge and LTTS show margin expansion to match.
M&A — the sector's real story
  • Coforge closed Encora, 23 April, consolidated from 1 May.
  • Persistent signed for Nagarro, expected to close Q4 CY26 or Q1 CY27.
  • Cyient closed Kinetic, with TAO Digital ($218m) pending August.
  • KPIT authorised $60–120m for Cymotive by mid-2029.
CPaaS
  • Tanla: gross profit and EBITDA grew faster than revenue — the right shape.
  • Tanla holds roughly 30% of the Indian CPaaS market; top 100 customers are over 50% of revenue.
  • Route Mobile processed 45.8bn billable transactions but margin fell to 9.5% from 11.9%.
  • Route Mobile flagged a security incident at its Masivian unit.

Act on these
Tanla
The board resolved on 14 July 2026 to put all future quarterly earnings calls on hold indefinitely. A profitable company withdrawing from analyst scrutiny is a governance flag on its own terms, whatever the numbers say. Separately contesting a ₹46.9cr tax demand on the Karix Mobile acquisition, with ₹8.9cr paid under protest.
Zensar
TCV fell 63% while revenue held. New deal wins are the leading indicator; revenue is the lagging one. This is the sector's clearest forward warning and it is not yet in the reported numbers.
KPIT
Pre-announced a miss on 30 June, ending a 23-quarter growth streak. Constant-currency growth of 0.1% YoY. Down 50% over one year and 49% year-to-date. Concentration in two large customers is the cause and has not been fixed.

Coforge
₹12,089cr of goodwill from Encora, purchase price allocation still provisional, funded by $550m of dollar debt. Organic growth of 1.1% is what the business does without acquisitions.
Cyient

Sector synthesis

The consensus entering FY27 was that Indian IT was recovering and AI would lift margins. Scored against the quarter.

Partly
That Indian IT is recovering. True in services — Persistent, Coforge and Happiest Minds all grew. Not true in ER&D, where KPIT went backwards and Cyient's core shrank. The recovery stopped at the enterprise door and did not reach engineering services.
Wrong
That AI is lifting margins broadly. Every management claimed AI benefit. Only Coforge (+414bps EBIT YoY) and LTTS (+200bps) show it in the numbers. Tata Elxsi fell 346bps and KPIT's EBIT is 12.3%. The claim is universal; the evidence is not.
Right
That scale would separate winners from losers. The two names buying scale — Coforge and Persistent — are two of the three positive one-year returns. The sub-scale names are the worst performers.
Wrong
That headline growth means anything this quarter. Coforge grew 49% and Cyient 21%, and both were acquisitions. Organic figures were 1.1% and −0.9%. Reading reported growth without stripping M&A produces the opposite conclusion to the correct one.
Right
That European automotive was the risk to watch. KPIT named two OEMs, one German and one Japanese. Tata Elxsi's margin fell. Prabhudas Lilladher cut FY28 growth forecasts on a slower European auto recovery. The single largest driver of dispersion this quarter was not AI — it was German car companies cutting R&D.

The call

Own now

Persistent Systems

The only company growing organically at scale without buying it. Revenue up 16.1% to $452.4m, 25 consecutive growth quarters, record $1.15bn TCV including a single deal above $650m, EBIT margin 16.0%. The sequential PAT dip is forex, not operations — EBIT rose 4.2% in the same quarter. It is up only 4.6% over a year, which for this delivery is the most reasonable price in the group.

Wait for organic proof

Coforge · LTTS

Coforge has the best order book in the sector at $2.23bn and margin genuinely ahead of guidance, but 1.1% organic growth and ₹12,089cr of provisional goodwill mean the thesis rests on Encora synergies that cannot yet be verified. Wait for a quarter where organic and reported converge. LTTS is the only ER&D name expanding margin, but it is still hostage to the same European auto cycle that broke KPIT.

Stay out

KPIT · Zensar · Tanla

KPIT's problem is customer concentration in a cutting industry, and nothing in the quarter fixed it. Zensar's 63% TCV decline is a forward warning that has not yet reached revenue. Tanla's numbers are good and its board has stopped taking questions — that combination is not one to own through an information vacuum.

What would make us more positive
  • Coforge reporting a quarter where organic cc growth exceeds 4%, confirming Encora is additive rather than substitutive.
  • Any sign of European OEM engineering budgets stabilising — this unlocks five of the eleven names at once.
  • Zensar's TCV recovering toward prior-year levels in Q2.
  • Tata Elxsi utilisation moving toward 80%, the condition management set for its own margin recovery.
What would make us cut
  • A second consecutive quarter of falling TCV at any services name. One quarter is noise; two is a trend that reaches revenue in three.
  • Coforge's goodwill moving from provisional to a write-down, or dollar debt cost rising.
  • KPIT losing a third large customer, which would make the concentration structural rather than cyclical.
  • Persistent's TCV falling back toward the $600m range seen in earlier quarters.

Caveats

  • This is three sectors, not one. IT services, ER&D and CPaaS run on different demand cycles and are analysed on different metrics. No ranking spans all eleven names, and the EBIT league table deliberately excludes the CPaaS pair.
  • The CPaaS framework was derived, not standard. The skill's metric map has no CPaaS entry. Gross margin and messaging volume were chosen as the core metrics because that is what the businesses earn on. Treat that framing as constructed for this report.
  • Tanla and Route Mobile were added at request and are not IT midcaps. They are included because they were asked for, and separated because merging them would have been misleading.
  • Reported growth is not comparable across this set. Coforge consolidates Encora from 1 May; Cyient consolidates Kinetic from 8 April; Tata Technologies' 33.8% includes acquisition. Constant-currency organic figures are the comparable ones and are shown wherever disclosed.
  • Some EBIT margins are on different bases. Coforge 16.0% consolidated versus 16.7% standalone organic. Cyient 9.7% is group including semiconductors. KPIT's 12.3% EBIT sits against a 16.2% EBITDA. Do not rank on small differences.
  • Tata Elxsi's margin decline is reported two ways. A 346bps sequential EBIT fall per broker commentary, and EBITDA at 21.2% against 22.27% a year earlier per the company. Both are shown; they measure different things.
  • Persistent, Coforge and KPIT are well covered here. Zensar, Route Mobile and Happiest Minds are thinner. Zensar's EBIT margin and Route Mobile's segment detail were not located at the granularity used for the larger names.
  • Price data is exchange-sourced; earnings data is not. Returns come from Kite on a single date. Earnings figures come from company releases and financial media via targeted search, not a full multi-source sweep.
  • Forward statements are projections. FY27 margin targets, growth guidance, deal-close timing and recovery expectations are management views, not outcomes.
