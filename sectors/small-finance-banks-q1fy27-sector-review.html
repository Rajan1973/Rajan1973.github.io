What management said

From the Q1 FY27 calls and releases (15–31 July 2026). Grouped by theme, because the seven banks are all fighting variations of the same battle.

Universal-bank ambitions
  • AU: filed in March 2026, and reported an in-principle RBI approval alongside its Q1 FY27 results — the only SFB to clear the bar so far.
  • Ujjivan: application returned by RBI in April 2026, with unsecured loans still 52% of the book.
  • Jana: application returned in October 2025 despite holding GNPA below 3% for two straight years and 72.5% secured assets.
  • RBI's message across both rejections: portfolio composition matters as much as the NPA print.
Microfinance & unsecured book
  • ESAF cut MFI/unsecured from 69% to 38% of advances in about a year — the fastest de-risking in the cohort.
  • Equitas is holding MFI deliberately at ~10% of advances through FY27.
  • Jana targets 10–12% MFI growth off a smaller base; management sees no material weather or geopolitical hit to the portfolio.
  • Utkarsh's classic JLG book grew just 5% YoY while its non-JLG micro-banking loans surged 93% — diversifying inside the "micro" book itself.
  • Sector context: MFIN says stress is "past the peak," roughly six to seven months before its February 2026 statement — but flags a funding winter as the real constraint on recovery.
Funding cost & NIM
  • Equitas: cost of funds rose QoQ to 7.05%; NIM compressed to 7.24% from 7.36%.
  • Ujjivan: cost of funds down 71bps YoY to 6.9%, NIM held flat at a sector-best 8.5%.
  • Utkarsh: cost of funds still the highest in the cohort at 7.7%, down only ~40bps YoY — a legacy of its weaker credit profile.
  • AU's scale gives it a funding-cost advantage none of the smaller six can match, even though it shows up as the lowest NIM in the table.
Growth composition
  • Jana: gold loans +113% YoY, vehicle loans +78%, Micro-LAP slowed sharply to +5% — a visible mix shift.
  • Ujjivan is expanding gold-loan branches from ~430 to ~575 by year-end, a direct secured-mix play.
  • Utkarsh's secured share rose to 51% from 45% YoY, targeting ~55% — but not until its own FY28 guidance.
  • AU's disbursements grew 42% YoY across an already-diversified vehicle/SBL/home-loan/credit-card book.
Capital actions
  • ESAF raised ₹85cr of Tier II capital in Q1; CRAR now 23.9%.
  • Utkarsh plans a ₹500cr Tier 2 NCD issue and is redeeming a ₹195cr, 12.5%-coupon tranche in mid-August — a ~₹20cr/yr saving and a ~250bps CRAR lift.
  • Utkarsh also has a holding-company amalgamation before the NCLT, hearing scheduled 6 August 2026.
  • AU's Tier I at 17.1% and CRAR at 18.9% give it room to absorb the CRR/SLR obligations a universal-bank licence will bring.

Red flags

Ranked by how much they should change position sizing, not by how alarming they sound.

Act on these
Utkarsh
Still loss-making (−₹34cr) with the highest GNPA in the cohort (5.9%) and loan growth of just 2.0% YoY. A holding-company amalgamation is before the NCLT on top of the operating repair. This is years behind the other six, not one quarter behind.
Suryoday
GNPA still 6.6%, second-highest in the cohort, and NNPA% was not disclosed in the Q1 release. About 98% of the Inclusive Finance book sits under CGFMU guarantee cover — the guarantee, not organic recovery, is doing real work in keeping reported credit cost down.
Ujjivan & Jana
Both had universal-bank applications returned by RBI despite hitting their own asset-quality benchmarks (sub-3% GNPA for two years at Jana; steady improvement at Ujjivan). The multi-year re-rating catalyst both stocks are partly priced for is now pushed out with no confirmed timeline.
Monitor next quarter
ESAF
The turnaround is real, but MFI/unsecured exposure was cut from 69% to 38% of advances in barely a year — a violently fast de-risking. CARE's outlook upgrade to Stable is eight days old as of this report (2 September 2026).
Equitas
NIM already compressing QoQ (7.36%→7.24%) against a management-guided "sustainable ~7%" floor, with cost of funds still rising QoQ. Cost-to-income remains elevated at 68.4%.
AU
The universal-bank transition is an execution project, not a free re-rating — CRR/SLR and priority-sector obligations change once the licence converts, and the bank has never operated under them.
Note and move on
Sector-wide
Every GNPA figure in this report improved YoY, but against an unusually stressed FY25–26 base — MFIN's own characterisation of the worst microfinance stress cycle in years. A beat against that base is not the same as absolute health for the two names still above 5% GNPA.
Jana
Micro-LAP growth slowed to just 5% YoY even as gold and vehicle loans surged 78–113% — a quiet mix shift toward more commoditised, lower-margin secured products.

The call

Own now

AU Small Finance Bank

The only SFB with in-principle universal-bank approval, 37% profit growth, the best GNPA print in the cohort, and disbursements up 42% YoY. The growth-versus-de-risking trade-off that defines the rest of the sector barely applies here — AU never carried a large MFI book after the Fincare merger diluted it further.

Confirm the trend before sizing up

Equitas · Ujjivan · ESAF · Jana

Genuine turnarounds across all four — two flipped from loss to profit, one trebled PAT. But each carries an open question: NIM compression at Equitas, an unresolved universal-bank ambition at Ujjivan and Jana, and a ratings upgrade at ESAF that is eight days old. One or two more clean quarters, not this one alone, should decide position size.

Stay out except at deep value

Utkarsh · Suryoday

Both still post the two highest GNPA numbers in the cohort and lean on CGFMU guarantee claims to keep credit cost presentable. Utkarsh's loan book has essentially stalled at +2% YoY with an NCLT amalgamation still pending. Neither has earned the growth re-rating the other five are starting to get.

What would make us more positive
  • A second SFB clearing RBI's universal-bank bar — Ujjivan's unsecured share falling toward 40% or below would be the tell.
  • Utkarsh's loan book re-accelerating past 15–20% YoY without a GNPA relapse.
  • Sector-wide credit costs falling further as CGFMU claims taper — the signal that repair is organic, not guarantee-subsidised.
What would make us cut
  • Any single quarter where sector-average GNPA rises again — it would undercut the entire "past the peak" thesis this report leans on.
  • AU losing its in-principle universal-bank approval, or missing final RBI conditions on the way to full conversion.
  • A reversal in CASA/retail-deposit growth across the cohort — this quarter's NIM resilience rests on falling cost of funds, which depends on retail deposits continuing to out-grow bulk funding.

Caveats

  • Universe built from web search. Small Finance Banks is not in the skill's sector-universe reference file. An eighth listed SFB, Capital Small Finance Bank (CAPITALSFB, ~₹1,236cr market cap), was not identified during phase 1 and is not covered in this report — it can be added on request.
  • Metric map is derived, not standard. Small Finance Banks is also not in the skill's sector-metrics library. NIM% was chosen as the core unit economic, advances/AUM growth vs GNPA change as the scissor pair, and CRAR/PCR in place of net debt/EBITDA as the leverage measure — a banking-standard framework built for this report, not drawn from a pre-existing map.
  • Several cells are marked n/d because the bank did not disclose the figure in its Q1 FY27 release or earnings call: Ujjivan/ESAF/Suryoday credit cost, Jana/Suryoday CASA ratio, Suryoday NIM and NNPA%. None of these were estimated.
  • AU's universal-bank timeline shows some source inconsistency. One report suggested in-principle approval as early as August 2025; AU's own press releases describe filing in March 2026 and reporting the in-principle approval alongside Q1 FY27 results. This report follows the company's own releases.
  • Sector-average GNPA figures (used in "the pressure point" and "Was MFIN right?") are simple equal-weighted averages across the 7 companies in this report, not size-weighted, and not the full 11-bank SFB universe that MFIN and CareEdge track at the industry level.
  • "Promised" in the walk-the-talk table is not uniformly a verified prior-quarter number. Where the specific Q4 FY26 guidance could not be independently sourced (Ujjivan, Jana, Utkarsh, Suryoday), the promised column reflects the trajectory implied by each bank's FY26-exit commentary rather than a quoted figure — flagged in that section's note.
  • Price data is single-sourced to Kite, per the skill's requirement, and spot-checked against Screener.in for AU Bank and against public reporting for Utkarsh; no discrepancies were found large enough to suggest an unadjusted corporate action or a wrong instrument token.
  • Valuation multiples are out of scope for this report. Kite carries no fundamentals (no P/E, market cap or EPS), and standard bank valuation (P/B, P/E vs RoE) was not sourced independently this cycle — the KPI table and price table should not be read as a valuation call.
