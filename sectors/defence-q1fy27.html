The structural split the returns are pricing

Private vs PSU — five names above +50% in one year, five names negative

MTAR (+336%), Paras (+107%), Data Patterns (+78%), Astra (+55%), Solar (+54%) are all private or mixed-commercial. BDL (−26%), Cochin (−24%), Mazagon (−20%), GRSE (−6%), BEML (−9%) are all defence PSUs. The Nifty Defence Index hit a fresh 52-week high on 13 August 2026 — the index is being driven by the private names while the PSU shipbuilders correct from peak valuations. Same defence budget, very different stock outcomes depending on execution certainty and order-book conversion speed.

Order book / revenue — the execution league

Order book divided by trailing revenue gives a coverage multiple. Higher is more visibility; above 10× is usually PSU-timing risk, not structural strength. Private names cluster at 2–5×, which converts faster.

The pressure point: HAL's EBITDA quality

Revenue grew 14%. EBITDA grew 47%. Margin jumped 610bps.

That spread is unusual for a manufacturing business where costs are largely fixed. The metric map says hunt for provision write-backs flattering PSU margins. HAL's prices are still provisional — "FPQ sales provisionally recognized using previous years' prices due to ongoing price finalization." InCred has turned cautious on earnings quality. The order book at ₹2.54 lakh crore is real; what portion of Q1 EBITDA is also real is the question the next few quarters will answer.

Red flags

Ranked by effect on position sizing, not by how alarming they sound.

Act on these
HAL — earnings quality
Revenue +14.4%, EBITDA +46.9%. The 610bps margin expansion is provisional: FY26 and Q1 FY27 prices are not yet finalised, no escalation has been applied, and prices are booked at prior-year rates. InCred turned cautious post-results. The order book at ₹2.54 lakh crore is real and the long-term thesis is intact; the Q1 margin number is not yet confirmed economics.
Mazagon Dock — order book declining without P75I
P75I submarine award has been expected "soon" for several quarters. Without it, Mazagon's order book has been declining from its peak. HDFC Securities cut TP to ₹2,625. The company is down 20% over one year despite PAT +21.5% — the market is already pricing the delay. Management aspirational target of ₹1 lakh crore order book and current trajectory are inconsistent.
BEL — material cost surge
Material costs rose 55% YoY in Q1, while revenue grew 25%. EBITDA margin fell to 25.83% from 28%+ guided. Management attributes this to product mix (different project mix vs guidance assumptions). If this is product mix it reverses; if it is input cost inflation at a specific supplier it does not. One data point — watching Q2 to confirm.
Monitor next quarter
BDL — base year conflict
PAT growth reported as +547% by one source (vs ₹18cr base in Q1 FY26) and +5% by another (vs ₹113cr). BDL's revenue is lumpy by nature — missile deliveries are all-or-nothing. The right comparison depends on what actually happened in Q1 FY26, which this report cannot independently confirm. Both figures are cited; treat the headline with caution.
ideaForge — supply chain risk
Global supply chain disruptions and component availability flagged as challenges since Q4 FY26. Company says it will execute FY27 opening order book by Q3 — but the stock is down 15.6% over 3 months and 16.1% month-to-date. The turnaround (first positive EBITDA) is real; the execution risk is also real.
MTAR — T2T trading restriction and valuation
MTAR trades in the Trade-to-Trade (BE) segment on NSE — each buy must result in delivery, no intraday. Volume on 31 July 2026 was only 108,126 shares. The 335.7% one-year return is extraordinary; it may also reflect liquidity constraints compressing the float. The Kaiga order and Thales/GKN qualifications are structurally positive; the valuation leaves no room for execution slip.
Note and move on
Astra Microwave — Q1 timing
Revenue −10.7%, EBITDA −13.7%. Order book ₹2,849cr. Company and management attribute this explicitly to execution timing. FII stake increased 7.56% in Q4 FY26 despite weak print — institutional money is betting on the order book converting, not on the quarterly trend.
Cochin Shipyard — PAT down 19%
Revenue +2%, PAT ₹151cr (−19%). Only PSU shipbuilder to post a PAT decline. Higher provisions and cost structure vs a weak revenue quarter. Down 23.6% over one year.
BEML still loss-making
Net loss ₹27cr, though improved sharply from ₹64cr. First positive Q1 EBITDA in 10 years (₹3cr). The direction is right; the destination is not yet here. Defence contributed ₹581cr of ₹1,181cr total order inflow.

Sector synthesis

The consensus entering FY27: defence PSUs would continue re-rating on order book visibility, private players would follow as indigenisation deepened. Scored.

Partly
That defence PSUs would continue re-rating. HAL is up only 3.4% in a year. BEL +1.3%. Mazagon −20%, BDL −26%. The PSU primes had their re-rating in FY24–25; the market is now asking about earnings quality (HAL), cost control (BEL), and order book renewal (Mazagon). The consensus was a year too late.
Right
That private players would outperform on indigenisation. MTAR +336%, Paras +107%, Data Patterns +78%, Astra +55%. The indigenisation mandate, DAC approval of sub-system level contracts, and PSU primes awarding sub-orders to private vendors all confirmed. This is structural, not a one-quarter trade.
Wrong
That order book size is the right metric for stock selection. HAL has a ₹2.54 lakh crore order book and returned +3.4%. MTAR has a ₹5,143cr order book and returned +336%. The market is pricing execution certainty and margin quality, not order book headline. A large PSU order book with slow conversion is not the same thing as a private player's deliverable backlog.
Right
That naval shipbuilding valuations were unsustainable. Mazagon Dock and Cochin Shipyard peaked in FY25 on expectations of sustained contract flow. Both are down more than 20% from those highs. The P75I delay has exposed the execution timing risk that always existed but was ignored at peak valuation.
Partly
That Solar Industries would benefit from the defence ramp. PAT +89%, stock +8.5% on results, order book ₹21,350cr, FY27 guidance ₹14,000cr. But Solar is 60%+ commercial explosives — calling this a defence re-rating is accurate only for the growing segment, not the company as a whole. +53.9% over a year is real; how much is defence vs commodity explosives pricing is not separately disclosed.

The call

Own now

GRSE · Zen Technologies

GRSE got Navratna status on 19 June, commissioned three naval ships in the quarter, and delivered PAT +44% on revenue +39%. Down 6% over a year despite all of that — the market is pricing it as a Mazagon clone waiting for order book decline. It is not: the Navy's next ordering cycle (NGOPV, ASW craft, research vessels) plus new commercial/export contracts and the Navratna capex freedom make this a different setup than Mazagon. Zen Technologies has ₹1,239cr of orders, ₹1,217cr of cash, no debt, and just launched four new products in one quarter including an AI anti-drone system and a UGV. Revenue missed on timing, not demand. The ₹295cr MoD simulator order post-quarter is the lead indicator.

Right thesis, execution to verify

HAL · BEL · Solar Industries

HAL has the largest defence order book in India and the correct strategic position — but Q1 margin needs confirmation once prices are finalised. BEL's 25% revenue growth is real, the order book is the strongest in the sector at 13×, and management's ≥28% margin guidance for FY27 only needs one more quarter to either confirm or break. Solar Industries is executing a genuine defence pivot alongside a strong commercial explosives business — but the segment split is not disclosed, making valuation guesswork. All three are worth owning; all three need one more quarter to resolve the specific question above each of them.

Stay out at current price

MTAR · Mazagon Dock · BDL

MTAR had an extraordinary quarter and the Kaiga order, Thales qualification and SLB data-centre business are structurally positive. But up 336% in a year with T2T liquidity restrictions and ₹7,312 CMP — a single order slip buys a −30% week. The entry point does not exist anymore. Mazagon Dock's order book is declining and P75I has been "imminent" for long enough that the next credible catalyst is the actual award, not the expectation. BDL is down 26% in a year with a disputed base-year PAT comparison and orders lumped into specific delivery quarters — the entry is right only when the next delivery cohort is confirmed and priced.

What would make us more positive
  • P75I submarine award to Mazagon Dock — single biggest unlock for the PSU shipbuilder. It reverses the order book decline and sets up a multi-year revenue ramp.
  • HAL price finalisation for FY26 and Q1 FY27 contracts — converts provisional EBITDA into confirmed economics and resolves the earnings quality question.
  • BEL Q2 EBITDA margin at or above 27% — confirms the Q1 product-mix explanation rather than structural cost inflation.
  • Zen Technologies converting its ₹177.5cr July simulator order to revenue in Q2 — validates management's timing explanation for the Q1 miss.
What would make us cut
  • A second consecutive HAL quarter with provisionally priced revenue and EBITDA margin above 26% — at that point the provisional pricing looks structural, not transient.
  • P75I award going to a different shipbuilder or being deferred into FY28 — strips Mazagon of its medium-term story.
  • MTAR order execution slipping — a single missed delivery on the Kaiga or Thales supply chain would reprice a stock with no margin for error at ₹7,312.
  • ideaForge supply chain disruption extending into Q3 without resolution — pushes the positive EBITDA achievement back and risks the FY27 guidance window.

Caveats

  • HAL's Q1 EBITDA margin is provisional. Prices for FY26 deliveries and Q1 FY27 are not yet finalised. Revenue is booked at prior-year rates with no escalation applied. The margin improvement may or may not survive the final price determination.
  • BDL's PAT growth figure is disputed across sources. One source reports +547% YoY (vs ₹18cr base in Q1 FY26), another +5% (vs ₹113cr). Both are cited in the report. The higher growth figure appears in more outlets and may reflect Q1 FY26 having been exceptionally weak. Do not use either without checking the primary exchange filing.
  • Solar Industries is a mixed commercial/defence business. Commercial explosives for mining are 60%+ of revenue. The order book of ₹21,350cr and FY27 guidance of ₹14,000cr are group-level. The defence segment share is not separately broken out in the quarterly filing.
  • MTAR Technologies trades in the T2T (BE) segment. Price discovery may be affected by delivery obligation requirements. Its returns should be interpreted with that caveat.
  • The private-vs-PSU return split is over one year. It tells you about valuation reset, not necessarily about next-year earnings. PSU order books are real and large; the question is conversion speed and margin quality on conversion.
  • Research depth is uneven. HAL, BEL, Mazagon, Solar, GRSE, MTAR and Zen Technologies have earnings call or investor presentation detail. BDL, Cochin Shipyard, BEML, Data Patterns, Astra Microwave and Paras Defence are covered from results releases and wire reports without full transcripts. ideaForge has only a press release — no PAT figure is confirmed, only positive EBITDA.
  • ideaForge PAT is not confirmed. The company disclosed revenue of ₹68.6cr and positive EBITDA for Q1 FY27. No PAT figure was located in the sources reviewed.
  • Forward statements are projections. Order inflow targets, revenue guidance, P75I award timelines and margin recovery estimates are management views, not outcomes.
