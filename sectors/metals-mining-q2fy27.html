Metals & Mining
Q2 FY27

Two different businesses reported this quarter. Non-ferrous producers earned record margins from metal prices. Steelmakers earned record volumes while the West Asia conflict pushed up coking coal, freight and fuel costs. Every steel call guided costs higher again for Q3. The PSUs that guided costs down last quarter got it wrong.

What management said

From the Q2 FY27 calls, presentations and releases. Grouped by theme.

Costs into Q3
  • Tata Steel: coking-coal consumption cost +$15/t in Q3 for India and the Netherlands.
  • Jindal Steel: +$20–25/t coking coal in Q3, after +$12–15 in Q2. Freight adds $4–5/t, and up to $20 more if sanctions persist.
  • SAIL: imported coal ₹23,500–23,900/t in Q3, falling to ~₹22,000 in Q4.
  • Hindalco: aluminium cost +5% again in Q3, with relief in Q4 as coal supply normalises.
  • NALCO: CP coke rising to ₹70,600/t in Q3 from ₹44,600 in FY26.
Prices
  • Tata Steel guides India Q3 realisation +₹3,500–3,600/t and UK +£28–30/t.
  • SAIL: October long and flat prices ~₹4,000/t above the Q2 average.
  • Hindalco: the MJP premium falls by more than $100 in Q3, so the Q2 export-premium benefit will not repeat.
  • NALCO sees alumina at $370–380/t near term, and no higher than ~$350 in FY28 as a global surplus builds.
Guidance changes
  • Hindustan Zinc cut FY27 zinc cost of production to $825–850/t, from $975–1,000.
  • Jindal Stainless cut volume growth to 4–6% (from 7–9%) and raised EBITDA/t to ₹20,000–22,000.
  • SAIL cut FY27 sales to ~21 mt from 22 mt, and that includes low-margin RINL trading.
  • Lloyds Metals cut FY27 copper to 5,850 t from 8,000 t. NALCO trimmed alumina to 2.45 mt.
Trade and imports
  • Tata Steel: Chinese exports are above 10 mt a month and heading past 100 mt this year.
  • Jindal Stainless: Chinese 200-series imports surged after the QCO suspension, which runs to March 2027.
  • UK flat-steel inventories are ~60% above normal, so Tata Steel UK breakeven has moved to Q4.
  • EU quotas and CBAM are limiting exports at Jindal Stainless and Jindal Steel.
Balance sheets
  • JSW Steel: net debt ₹44,482 cr, from ₹79,153 cr a year ago. Moody's upgraded it to investment grade.
  • Hindustan Zinc: net cash ₹11,268 cr. Q2 free cash flow before growth capex ₹6,389 cr.
  • SAIL: ₹1.2 lakh cr of capex over FY27–31, with an FPO under consideration.
  • Novelis leverage peaks at ~4x in Q4. Lloyds' consolidated net debt is below ₹20,000 cr after Chemaf.
Capacity
  • JSW: Dolvi Phase-III to 15 mtpa by September 2027. BF-3 at ~90% utilisation.
  • NALCO: its 5th-stream 1 mt refinery starts in late December or January.
  • Jindal Steel: a 192 km slurry pipeline saves ~₹700/t on freight. Its Angul furnace targets 12,000 tpd by December.
  • Hindalco: over ₹50,000 cr of India projects under execution.

Red flags

Ranked by how much they should change position sizing, not by how alarming they sound.

Act on these
NMDC
Production rose 19%, but sales fell 2% and PAT fell 2%. About 31 lakh tonnes of inventory built up in H1, mostly at KIOM. A ₹1,430 cr stock-adjustment credit in H1 supported EBITDA. Royalty and levies take ~43% of revenue.
SAIL
It guided coal costs down by ₹1,000–2,000/t, and they rose ₹1,700. It cut its volume guide. ₹1.2 lakh cr of capex is coming, with a possible FPO. Wage-revision provisions are still unquantified.
Jindal Saw
Consolidated EBITDA fell 35% YoY. PAT of ₹35 cr includes a ₹27 cr tax credit. Gross profit per kg fell to ₹41 from ₹51. The Saudi order is stalled by the war.
NLC India
Reported PAT of ₹1,328 cr came from a negative tax charge. PBT halved to ₹465 cr as interest rose 51%. Underlying profit fell.
Monitor next quarter
Hindalco
It began capitalising pot relining, which adds ~₹130 cr a quarter to EBITDA (plus a Q1 catch-up). Copper included ₹350–400 cr of one-offs. Novelis leverage is heading to ~4x.
Tata Steel
The Board deferred any change to the Netherlands carrying value until March 2027. UK breakeven has slipped again, to Q4.
Shyam Metalics
PAT +96% is helped by a depreciation change from WDV to SLM. Operating EBITDA +53% is the cleaner number.
Ratnamani
H1 operating cash flow was negative and inventory and receivables spiked. US delivered-duty-paid exports gross up revenue and compress the reported margin.
APL Apollo
It stopped disclosing product-level EBITDA. Gross profit per tonne fell ₹500 QoQ.
Note and move on
Vedanta group
The YoY base is broken by the demerger. Sources disagree on Vedanta Iron & Steel's EBITDA (₹48 cr vs ₹83 cr). No Q2 data was found for Vedanta Oil & Gas.
Lloyds Metals
It cut copper guidance and its steel plant is a quarter late. Chemaf's debt restructuring is still open.
Coal India
H1 production is ~39% of the 815 mt target, so H2 has to accelerate. It booked a ₹499 cr wage provision.

The call

Own now

Hindustan Zinc · JSW Steel · Vedanta Aluminium

Hindustan Zinc cut its cost guidance by ~$150/t and holds ₹11,268 cr of net cash. Silver is 45% of its profit, so size it as a silver position too. JSW has the best balance-sheet repair in the sector: net debt down 44% YoY and an investment-grade rating. Vedanta Aluminium earned a 46% margin on record output at 0.7x leverage. All three are price-exposed, so these are cycle positions, not compounders.

Accumulate on evidence

Tata Steel · Jindal Steel · Hindalco · Jindal Stainless · Shyam Metalics

All five executed, but each needs Q3 price to beat Q3 coal. Tata: Netherlands carrying value and UK breakeven. Jindal Steel: 6 mt in H2 to hit 11 mt. Hindalco: earnings net of pot relining and copper one-offs. Jindal Stainless: anti-dumping duty by December. Shyam: margin on a like-for-like depreciation basis.

Stay out

NMDC · SAIL · Jindal Saw · NLC

NMDC is building inventory, not selling it. SAIL missed its own cost guidance and faces an FPO overhang. Jindal Saw's margin halved, and its profit relies on a tax credit. NLC's profit is a tax line. Revisit NMDC when sales catch up with production, and SAIL when Q3 EBITDA per tonne clears ₹10,000. The market disagrees on Jindal Saw: it is up 55% over a year and 15% since 30 October. That price pays for the order book before the margin has recovered.

What would make us more positive
  • Spot coking coal back below $250/t while domestic HRC holds ₹58,000+.
  • A DGTR anti-dumping duty on Chinese stainless and flat steel by December.
  • Hormuz shipping normalising, which unlocks NALCO, APL Dubai and Jindal Saw Saudi.
What would make us cut
  • Chinese exports above 110 mt for the year, pushing domestic prices down.
  • A silver or zinc reversal: about 45% of Hindustan Zinc's H1 profit was silver.
  • Further PSU cost-guidance misses, or the SAIL FPO pricing at a discount.

Caveats

  • All 26 tracked companies have reported. Vedanta Oil & Gas results could not be found in any source, so its row is n/d.
  • EBITDA per tonne is mostly derived.
    • Disclosed: Tata Steel India (₹17,257), Jindal Steel (₹10,529), Lloyds (by product) and APL Apollo (~₹6,000).
    • Analyst-cited on the call: SAIL (~₹8,170).
    • Derived by us: JSW (~₹13,300), Shyam (~₹5,300), NMDC (~₹2,300) and Vedanta Aluminium (~₹1.57 lakh, on production rather than sales).
  • The scissor's per-tonne changes are mostly derived from EBITDA growth and volume growth. JSW's YoY uses pro-forma FY26 figures that exclude BPSL.
  • The demerger breaks Vedanta's base. Revenue is ₹26,356 cr in compiled filings and ₹25,812 cr in the press release. The residual entity's scope after June 2026 is unclear from the sources.
  • Jindal Stainless's transcript does not say whether ₹2,762 cr EBITDA and ₹1,666 cr PAT are Q2 or H1. We treat them as H1 and do not score them.
  • Some sources were stale. For Welspun Corp, NLC, GMDC, Hindustan Copper, MIDHANI, NMDC Steel, Sandur and Coal India we used filed results only (compiled by Tapetide), with no call. Their commentary sections are thin.
  • Results dates differ from the tracker for two names. Filings show NLC on 5 Nov and GMDC on 2 Nov. The tracker had 2 Nov and 30 Oct.
