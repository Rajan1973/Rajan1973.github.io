Why this is split

Ranking Hindalco against Coal India against APL Apollo on one metric produces a tidy table that means nothing. Each group runs on a different commodity cycle, a different cost driver, and a different forward signal.

The pressure point: Coal India's inventory trap

Production versus offtake — the gap that cannot persist

Coal India produced 169.6MT and sold 197.7MT. The 28MT gap came from drawing inventories down 22%. It can do this once. The July read confirms the problem: production -15.6% YoY, offtake -11.3%.

What management said

Q2 — every steelmaker guided down
  • Tata Steel: India NSR expected ₹1,500/t lower in Q2; coking coal at $184/t (+$10/t from Q1).
  • JSW Steel: coking coal cost up $12–15/t in Q2. Domestic demand remains steady.
  • Lloyds Metals: iron ore segment will moderate as Chemaf integration continues.
  • The guided-down-Q2 signal is unanimous across steelmakers for the first time in three quarters.
Non-ferrous — the quarter they don't want to talk about going away
  • Hindalco: Middle East supply disruption lifted LME aluminium significantly; management did not quantify how much of the record EBITDA is structural versus cyclical.
  • Hindustan Zinc: new CEO Amarendu Prakash (ex-SAIL CMD) took charge. Hot Acid Leaching technology and a 510KTPA fertilizer project cited as growth vectors.
  • Hindalco: US tariff impact $70mn per quarter being managed through supply chain adjustments and hybrid US copper production (-16% volume on maintenance).
Coal India — the word nobody said
  • Management cited monsoon disruption and land acquisition delays for the production miss.
  • Nobody used the word inventory to describe the 28MT production-offtake gap.
  • JPMorgan cut its target price post-results; the market is pricing the production problem, the company is not acknowledging it in those terms.
M&A and capital deployment
  • Lloyds Metals: capex of ₹3,005cr in one quarter; Chemaf acquisition added ₹19,000cr consol net debt.
  • Shyam Metallics: ₹4,500cr fundraise approved — 46% of the current market cap.
  • JSW Steel: FY27 target of 28.6MT sales, export mix at 46% as domestic realisations lagged.
Vedanta demerger — what the numbers cannot say
  • Continuing-operations revenue +53.6% to ₹24,205cr; total revenue -11.4% to ₹33,515cr — same company, same quarter, opposite signs.
  • VEDALUM PAT +216% to net margin 31%; VEDSTEEL turned profitable ₹121cr vs prior loss.
  • VEDOILGAS PAT ₹945cr includes a slump-sale gain; continuing operations lost ₹152cr.
  • None of these figures are comparable to any prior period. Treat as first-quarter-of-existence data, not YoY growth.

Red flags

Ranked by effect on position sizing, not by how alarming they sound.

Act on these
Coal India — production trend
Q1 production fell 7.5% to 169.6MT, the lowest Q1 in recent years. Offtake of 197.7MT was maintained by drawing inventories down 22%. That is a one-time lever. July production was -15.6% YoY. The company cannot sustain offtake above production without exhausting buffer stock. Revenue and dividend guidance implicitly assume production recovers — there is no evidence it will in Q2.
Shyam Metallics — ED provisional attachment
The Enforcement Directorate issued a provisional attachment order of ₹152cr on subsidiary Shyam SEL on allegations of coal pilferage. This is not a civil dispute — it is a criminal investigation at the subsidiary level. The ₹4,500cr fundraise approved in the same period sits uncomfortably alongside an unresolved enforcement action.
Vedanta complex — non-comparable base
Every YoY figure for VEDL, VEDALUM, VEDSTEEL and VEDOILGAS is mathematically meaningless because the prior-year base includes the businesses that were demerged. Continuing-operations revenue rose 53.6% and total revenue fell 11.4% in the same company in the same quarter. Any model built off the reported growth rates is wrong by construction.
Monitor next quarter
Steel — Q2 coking coal lag
JSW guided +$12-15/t on coking coal in Q2. Tata guided $184/t and India NSR -₹1,500/t. The two move in the same direction. EBITDA/t compression in Q2 is not a risk — it is already guided. The question is whether volumes hold, which depends on Chinese export competition and domestic construction demand.
Lloyds Metals — Chemaf leverage
Standalone EBITDA margin is a record 39.2% on captive iron ore. But the Chemaf acquisition added ₹19,000cr of consolidated net debt, against a standalone net debt of ₹5,616cr. The iron ore business is exceptional; the acquisition is a leverage event at a different scale. Consol and standalone are two different companies this quarter.
Hindalco — tariff drag per quarter
US tariff exposure quantified at $70mn per quarter — manageable at current aluminium prices, not if LME retraces. The Middle East supply disruption that boosted aluminium prices this quarter is not guaranteed to persist.
NMDC Steel — interest cost structure
Revenue +8.8% to ₹3,662cr and PAT only ₹50.51cr, doubling from a very low base but still thin. High interest charges dominate the P&L. The plant is running but not yet earning its cost of capital.
Note and move on
JSL — gas supply and Middle East logistics
Revenue +10.5% but volume -7% on gas supply constraints and Middle East shipping disruptions. The margin was held at EBITDA level; the volume loss is external and likely temporary. D/E 0.14x is the best balance sheet in steel.
Maharashtra Seamless — treasury PAT
PAT ₹271cr (+150% QoQ) is driven by treasury income, not operations. Revenue was only +2.8% QoQ. The headline number is misleading as an operating read.
Missing coverage
NMDC, NLC India, Sandur Manganese, GMDC, Ratnamani, Jindal Saw and MIDHANI — seven of twenty names — have no Q1 FY27 data in the sources reviewed. Their CMPs and AMFI cap tiers are in the returns table; everything else is absent.

Sector synthesis

The consensus entering FY27 was that metals were in a multi-year capex upcycle and non-ferrous would be the winner. Scored against the quarter.

Right
That non-ferrous would outperform. Hindalco's EBITDA up 73%, PAT up 75%, all-time record. Hindustan Zinc PAT up 145%. Middle East supply disruption did more work this quarter than anyone modelled, but the structural thesis — that aluminium is scarcer than steel — held.
Wrong
That Coal India's dividend and volume would sustain. Production -7.5% in the quarter, -15.6% in July. Inventory drawn 22%. The dividend (₹5.50/share) is real; the production trajectory is not. Anyone modelling flat-to-rising offtake built on a false assumption.
Right
That the pipes and products segment would decouple from steel. Welspun Corp up 159.7% over a year, order book at ₹24,750cr, net cash ₹2,336cr. APL Apollo EBITDA/t up 17.9% even as volume fell 6%. The spread businesses outran the commodity businesses by a factor of five on returns.
Partly
That captive ore gives durable margin advantage. Lloyds Metals EBITDA margin of 39.2% is the proof case. But Lloyds then levered itself through the Chemaf acquisition at ₹19,000cr consolidated net debt. The captive advantage is real; the capital allocation around it is a separate question.
Wrong
That Chinese dumping risk would ease in H1 FY26. JSW routed 46% of sales as exports in Q1 — not a choice, a concession to domestic realisation pressure from cheap imports. Tata's NSR guidance for Q2 (-₹1,500/t) reflects the same dynamic. The import competition problem is not resolved.

The call

Own now

Hindalco · Welspun Corp

Hindalco had the best quarter in the sector on an absolute basis — record EBITDA, record PAT, all segments at all-time highs, Novelis up 37%. The tariff drag of $70mn/quarter is real but priced; the non-ferrous structural story is not. Welspun is up 159.7% over a year on an all-time-high EBITDA quarter with ₹24,750cr of order book and net cash. The pipes-to-infrastructure theme is not a trade — it is a three-year re-rating that has years left in it.

Good business, guided-down entry

Tata Steel · Lloyds Metals · APL Apollo

Tata's India business is the best margin steel operation in the country at ₹19,162 EBITDA/t, but management has already told you Q2 will be worse on NSR and coking coal. The right entry is after the Q2 print confirms the dip. Lloyds has 39.2% EBITDA margin from captive iron ore — best in the sector — but ₹19,000cr consolidated net debt from Chemaf changes the risk profile. APL Apollo's EBITDA/t is rising even as volumes fall — the quality thesis is intact, but the stock moved 18.7% month-to-date and +31% over a year. All three deserve to be owned; none at today's price with a Q2 headwind in front of them.

Stay out

Coal India · Shyam Metallics (for now)

Coal India's dividend is defensible; its production trajectory is not. July -15.6% means Q2 will repeat the inventory draw or miss offtake. The company is earning on borrowed inventory. Shyam Metallics posted solid numbers — revenue +23%, margin +100bps — but the ED provisional attachment on Shyam SEL is not a civil dispute and the ₹4,500cr fundraise at current prices is a large dilution event alongside an unresolved enforcement action. Both facts resolve or don't resolve in the next two quarters.

What would make us more positive
  • Coal India Q2 production recovering toward 185MT+ without further inventory draw. Anything below that confirms the structural problem.
  • Chinese hot-rolled coil prices stabilising, which would ease the realisation pressure that is pushing JSW and Tata exports above 40% of sales.
  • Shyam Metallics ED attachment dropped or significantly reduced — removes the governance overhang from an otherwise solid operational story.
  • Lloyds Metals disclosing a standalone-versus-consolidated bridge so the Chemaf debt can be tracked separately from the iron ore business economics.
What would make us cut
  • Coal India Q2 offtake falling alongside production — confirming the buffer stock is exhausted and demand is also softening.
  • LME aluminium retracing more than 15% from Q1 levels, which would strip roughly half the Hindalco outperformance.
  • A second consecutive quarter of EBITDA/t compression in steel, which would mean the guided-down Q2 has no floor.
  • Shyam Metallics fundraise pricing below ₹800/share, implying the board believes current prices are unsustainable.

Caveats

  • This is five sectors in one report. Steel, non-ferrous, mining, products/pipes and the Vedanta complex each run on different cycles. No ranking spans all twenty names, and the EBITDA/t league table deliberately excludes every non-steel company.
  • Vedanta is excluded from all rankings. The demerger took effect 1 May 2026. Q1 FY27 contains one month of the discontinued entities in the base. Every YoY figure is arithmetically invalid. The four Vedanta entities appear in the returns table with their CMPs; they appear nowhere else.
  • Seven names have no Q1 FY27 earnings data. NMDC, NLC India, Sandur Manganese, GMDC, Ratnamani, Jindal Saw and MIDHANI are not in the earnings rankings. Their absence is marked, not estimated.
  • Ten names have CMP-only price data. The Kite session reached its call limit before historical anchors could be pulled for all twenty names. The ten names with full return data are those collected in a prior session and verified in this one; the ten CMP-only names have their earnings fully covered.
  • Lloyds Metals: standalone versus consolidated. The 39.2% EBITDA margin, the record production numbers and the ₹2,120cr EBITDA are all standalone figures. The ₹19,000cr consolidated net debt is from consolidating Chemaf. These are not contradictory — they are two different entities in the same filing.
  • Coal India inventory draw is an inference. The company disclosed production of 169.6MT and offtake of 197.7MT. The 28MT gap was labelled as inventory reduction based on the arithmetic; the company did not use the phrase "inventory draw" in its investor communication.
  • Hindalco's record EBITDA is partly Middle East-driven. The exact LME uplift from Middle East supply disruption was not quantified in public disclosures. The record is real; how much is structural versus cyclical is not separately disclosed.
  • Maharashtra Seamless PAT is not an operations read. The ₹271cr includes substantial treasury income. The operational revenue growth was +2.8% QoQ.
  • Research depth is uneven. Tata Steel, JSW Steel, Hindalco, Coal India, Lloyds Metals, Shyam Metallics and Welspun Corp are well covered from earnings calls and investor presentations. SAIL, JSL, APL Apollo and NMDC Steel are moderate. Seven names are absent.
  • Forward statements are projections. Coking coal guidance, NSR direction, production recovery targets and capex timelines are management views, not outcomes.
