What management said

From the Q2 FY27 calls, releases and presentations. Grouped by theme, because the companies said broadly the same things.

Demand
  • UltraTech puts Q2 industry demand at about 10% and says it grew faster.
  • UltraTech's regions: North high double digits, South 15–20%, West and Central ~10%, East ~5%.
  • Nuvoco guides H2 industry growth of 7–8%, its own about 8%.
  • ACC expects demand to strengthen after the monsoon.
Pricing
  • UltraTech: trade prices 1–1.5% lower QoQ, then a 7–10% per-bag rise in late September. October spot is ~5% above the Q2 average.
  • JK Cement took about ₹20/bag in late September and says ₹10–15 more is needed.
  • Dalmia's CFO expects price momentum to offset cost.
  • Birla Corp says Central is soft and it is putting market share ahead of price.
Cost — the key guidance
  • Ambuja: petcoke $170/t in September, $188 in October. A year ago it was $115.
  • Ambuja puts the geopolitical cost hit at ₹228/t, 77% of its EBITDA decline.
  • Nuvoco: energy cost ~₹1,080/t, rising ₹70–80/t in Q3. Fuel ₹1.59 → ~₹1.8 per '000 kcal.
  • Birla Corp: Q3 fuel up about ₹100/t.
  • JK Cement: cost per kcal heading toward ₹2, full impact by Q4.
Fuel stockpiles and debt
  • Nuvoco net debt is about ₹5,000 cr after petcoke stocking and capex. It expects to unwind this in H2.
  • Birla Corp's gross borrowings rose to ₹2,432 cr on working capital and petcoke inventory.
  • JK Lakshmi's net debt went from ₹1,266 cr to ₹2,027 cr in six months.
  • JK Cement's net debt rose 36% to ₹4,571 cr.
Capacity
  • UltraTech is at 204.7 MTPA domestic, on track for 240+ by March 2028.
  • Ambuja is at ~113.5 MTPA, with a further 5.6 MTPA due in H2. It targets 149 by FY29 and 155 by FY30.
  • ACC added 3.4 MTPA at Salai Banwa and Kalamboli.
  • Dalmia's 3.6 MTPA Belgaum clinker line is on trial run, ahead of schedule.
  • JK Cement targets 50 MT by FY30. Nuvoco's Surat 2 Mt grinding unit is commercial.
Green power & consolidation
  • UltraTech green power at 48%; JK Lakshmi 48%; ACC 34.4%.
  • ACC shareholders approved the Ambuja merger on 29 September with over 95% of votes. NCLT sanction is pending.
  • Orient's merger into Ambuja is also pending.
  • Dalmia's Bhilai Jaypee plan has creditor approval and awaits NCLT.
  • UltraTech launched its Ultravolt wires brand from Jhagadia.

Red flags

Ranked by how much they should change position sizing, not by how alarming they sound.

Act on these
ACC
EBITDA/t halved to ₹414. Management says the numbers are "not strictly comparable" because of supply arrangements (MSA) with parent Ambuja, a related-party channel that now shapes reported earnings. The year-ago PAT also carried ₹864 cr of tax items.
Sagar
EBITDA/t of ₹265 against a Q1-stated FY27 target of ₹500–550. Realisation fell 7% even as volume grew 26%, so the growth was bought. The loss widened to ₹52 cr and finance cost rose 19%.
JK Lakshmi
Net debt up ₹761 cr in six months to ₹2,027 cr, 2.31x EBITDA, while EBITDA/t fell 29% to ₹517. Quarterly volumes are not disclosed in the presentation.
Monitor next quarter
Dalmia Bharat
₹339 cr of exceptional charges: a ₹282 cr impairment, including three captive power plants and a discontinued brand, plus a ₹57 cr separation scheme. It follows a ₹182 cr charge in Q1. Two quarters of write-downs in a row.
Nuvoco
Net debt near ₹5,000 cr on petcoke stocking. The effective tax rate is 41%, and a decision between the old and new tax regimes is due by March.
JK Cement
Net debt/EBITDA of 1.95x, up from 1.69x in Q1, and still the highest of the majors. Capex runs at about ₹2,500 cr a year.
Orient Cement
Sources disagree on Q2 PAT: ₹77 cr in one, ₹32 cr in another. A tax-regime switch (Section 115BAA) and a useful-life reassessment both move the number. Wait for the filing.
Note and move on
Ambuja
The ₹998/t "adjusted" EBITDA is management's own construct. Reported cost was ₹4,410/t against a ₹4,250 FY27 target. Penna and Sanghi run at about 50% utilisation.
Shree Cement
India utilisation is only 56%. Volume growth of 18% is coming from new capacity that takes about three years to fill.
Birla Corp
EBITDA/t fell to ₹482 on clinker purchases after a planned shutdown. It says it is choosing share over price in Central.
Sector-wide
Several companies stocked high-priced petcoke, funded with debt. That carries the cost into Q3 and leaves balance sheets more stretched at the same time.

The call

Own now

UltraTech

The only scale player to grow EBITDA per tonne in the worst fuel quarter in two years, on 13.8% volume growth. It has 48% green power, October spot prices 5% above the Q2 average, and 240 MTPA on schedule. That is the hedge against exactly this cycle.

Accumulate after the Q3 print

Shree · JK Cement · Nuvoco

All three beat the preview and grew volume. All three will burn $170+ petcoke in Q3, and JK Cement and Nuvoco are carrying more debt to stock it. Buy them once the cost peak is visible in the numbers and the ₹15–20/bag hikes have held, not before.

Stay out

ACC · Sagar · JK Lakshmi

ACC's EBITDA per tonne halved, and its earnings now run through supply arrangements with its own parent. Sagar is loss-making at ₹265 a tonne, against a target of ₹500–550. JK Lakshmi added ₹761 cr of net debt in six months while its margin fell 29%.

What would make us more positive
  • The late-September hikes of ₹15–20/bag holding through December.
  • Imported petcoke back below $150/t.
  • Both together mean Q4 EBITDA/t recovers across the sector, not just at the leaders.
What would make us cut
  • Tier-2 discounting to defend share, as Birla already signals in Central.
  • UltraTech's derived EBITDA/t falling below ₹1,000 in Q3.
  • Leveraged names (JK Lakshmi, Nuvoco, JK Cement) still building debt in Q3 instead of releasing petcoke working capital.

Caveats

  • All 15 tracked companies have reported. Star Cement was the last, on 6 November.
  • Several EBITDA/t figures are derived. Reported by the company or its release: Ambuja, Shree, ACC, JK Cement, JK Lakshmi, Birla Corp, Sagar. Our calculation (EBITDA ÷ volume): UltraTech, Dalmia, Ramco, Nuvoco. Don't rank the derived ones against each other on small gaps.
  • Four companies have no per-tonne data. India Cements, Star, Heidelberg and Orient disclosed no quarterly volume in any source we found, so they are excluded from the scissor and the league table. Their operating profit lines are in the cards.
  • Operating profit is not always EBITDA. For Ramco, Star, Heidelberg and Orient the aggregators report "operating profit". The basis may differ from company-reported EBITDA.
  • YoY reads are distorted by M&A and accounting. Ambuja and ACC run intra-group supply arrangements. Ambuja's and ACC's year-ago PAT included large tax reversals. Dalmia booked exceptional charges in both quarters.
  • Source conflicts are flagged, not resolved. Orient's PAT is ₹77 cr or ₹32 cr depending on source. UltraTech's volume growth is 13.8% in the release and 12.9% in one article; the release is used.
  • The consensus comparison is a broker preview, Axis Securities' of 5 October 2026, not a post-result poll. It covers 8 companies.
  • Regional demand figures are UltraTech's own read of its markets, not industry data.
  • Forward statements are projections, not outcomes. That covers the Q3 cost guidance, capacity dates and merger timing.
