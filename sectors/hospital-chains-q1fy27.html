What management said

From the Q1 FY27 calls and releases. Grouped by theme, because the companies said broadly the same things.

Doctor & clinician costs
  • Max, Fortis, Rainbow and Jupiter all named rising professional fees as a margin driver, unprompted.
  • Fortis added a new ~₹31cr/quarter ESOP and clinician-retention cost line.
  • NH's fees jumped +34% QoQ to ₹327cr — management calls it a reclassification, not real cost growth.
  • Six of thirteen companies raised this theme this quarter alone.
New-hospital drag, and where it's closing
  • Medanta's Noida loss: ₹23.6cr → ₹4.9cr, ahead of H2FY27 breakeven guidance.
  • Apollo's digital health loss: ₹49cr → ~₹10cr.
  • Jupiter's Dombivli: ₹9.5cr loss this quarter, still open.
  • Shalby's Gurgaon: first EBITDA-positive quarter, PBT still negative.
Consolidation & ownership change
  • KKR completed a ~$400m, 51.59% buyout of HCG from CVC.
  • Aster's QCIL merger legally closed 1–2 July 2026 — after the quarter.
  • IHH's long-pending 26% Fortis open offer is SEBI-cleared but still not closed.
  • Aster/Advent reportedly in due diligence for a controlling stake in Yatharth — both parties deny it.
Leverage moving in both directions
  • Max quietly loosened its net debt/EBITDA ceiling from <1x to up to 2.5x.
  • KIMS: promoters put in ₹1,500cr of warrants after a ₹1,500cr QIP.
  • ICRA downgraded Shalby's long-term rating from A+ to A.
  • Yatharth's debt rose ₹210cr → ₹300cr within the quarter on expansion financing.
Regulatory pricing pressure
  • Fortis: 30% MRP discount mandate on CGHS/ECHS oncology pricing.
  • Shalby: NPPA knee-implant price cap extended to 15 Nov 2026.
  • Both hit specific high-margin service lines directly, not the sector broadly.

The call

Own now

Apollo Hospitals

Best print in the sector on every disclosed line — revenue, margin and PAT all beat, and the FY27 capex guide was raised, not walked back (₹3,185cr for 1,720 beds, from ~₹1,980cr for ~1,000). The HealthCo demerger, cleared by the NCLT, is a re-rating catalyst still ahead. Risk: its ARPOB is disclosed per-inpatient, not per-bed-day, so it isn't directly comparable to the rest of the league table.

Accumulate into the doctor-cost reset

Medanta · Max · Rainbow

Medanta's Noida drag is closing exactly on its own guided schedule and its India-hospitals margin is expanding, not contracting. Max's ARPOB pricing power (+5-9%) is intact even as leverage guidance loosens. Rainbow is debt-free and its margin cut (32% to 24-25%) is a guidance reset made in public, not a number quietly missed later. Buy the reset, not before it's priced in.

Stay out

Narayana Hrudayalaya · KIMS · Shalby

NH's margin collapse is a genuine mix effect compounded by a disputed cost reclassification and a 259%-combined-ratio insurance book. KIMS is on its second consecutive year of a Q1 margin/PAT miss despite strong topline. Shalby's headline PAT growth doesn't survive a look at the segment numbers, and its core ortho specialty just got a fresh regulatory price cap.

What would make us more positive
  • An independent read confirming NH's +34% QoQ doctor-fee jump was genuinely a reclassification, not a real cost increase.
  • A second quarter of narrowing new-hospital losses at Medanta, Apollo and Jupiter — proof the drag is temporary, not structural.
  • KIMS's Karnataka segment margin moving off -61.2% — the clearest sign the multi-state ramp is starting to pay for itself.
What would make us cut
  • Any hospital citing doctor or clinician cost inflation for a second consecutive quarter — this quarter's six become a trend, not a one-off.
  • Fortis specifically: adverse findings from the forensic audit, or a further slip in the IHH open-offer closing.
  • KIMS specifically: a third consecutive Q1 margin/PAT miss would confirm this is structural.

Caveats

  • ARPOB basis is not standardised. Apollo discloses per-inpatient revenue, and Aster and HCG disclose ARPP (per-patient) — fundamentally different units from the per-bed-day ARPOB used by the other ten. All three are excluded from the league table and scissor chart for that reason, not because they performed worse.
  • Two league-table figures are derived. Fortis and Narayana Hrudayalaya disclose ARPOB annually; the daily figures shown (marked †) are computed by dividing by 365, not company-reported.
  • Medanta's margin-change figure is derived, back-calculated from disclosed revenue and EBITDA growth rates rather than a directly stated prior-period margin.
  • The scissor chart covers 7 of 13 companies. Apollo, Aster, KIMS, HCG, Yatharth and Artemis lack a clean, YoY-comparable bed-count disclosure and are excluded rather than forced onto a mismatched proxy.
  • Aster's Q1FY27 numbers are statutory, Aster-standalone only. The QCIL merger legally closed 1–2 July 2026, after quarter-end. Every "combined" or "pro-forma" figure cited is an unaudited management construct, not a statutory number.
  • Beat/miss against consensus is confirmed for only 6 of 13 names — Apollo, Fortis, Aster (pro-forma), Rainbow, HCG and Jupiter, where a broker preview or estimate was found. The rest are assessed against the company's own prior guidance.
  • NH's YoY figures are distorted by UK consolidation. Practice Plus Group's first full quarter in the base explains most of the 78% consol revenue growth and the margin compression; India-hospitals-only figures are shown separately in the company card where disclosed.
  • Company marks in the card headers are ticker monograms, not logos.
