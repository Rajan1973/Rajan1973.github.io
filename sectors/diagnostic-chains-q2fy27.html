Sector earnings review · Quarter ended 30 September 2026

Diagnostic Chains
Q2 FY27

The best diagnostics quarter in two years, with part of it on loan. Every chain grew revenue double digits, and the incumbents widened margins on volume. But a government tariff revision (CGHS) added 2–3 points of growth at Dr Lal and Vijaya that will not repeat. The volume-for-price trade is visible at Krsnaa and Thyrocare, and it is costing them margin.

What management said

From the Q2 FY27 calls, releases and presentations. Grouped by theme.

Growth guidance
  • Dr Lal: FY27 revenue growth likely in the high teens. H1 at +19.4% is "slightly ahead of plan".
  • Vijaya: at least 20% growth for FY27 is achievable, and over 15% is sustainable for three to five years.
  • Thyrocare raised FY27 revenue guidance to the "early 20s".
  • Entero says it is on track to deliver "comfortably" against full-year guidance.
Price and mix
  • Vijaya's H1 growth split: ~16% volume, 4–5% test mix, ~3% CGHS. Historical price realisation compounds at only ~2%.
  • Dr Lal: revenue per patient +9.7% to ₹976, with 2–3 points from CGHS.
  • Metropolis: revenue per patient +6% to ₹1,221 and per test +5%. Price increases are planned.
  • Krsnaa: retail revenue per patient fell to ₹904 from ₹967.
Margins
  • Vijaya is "fairly confident" of 42%+ EBITDA for FY27. H1 was 43–44%.
  • Metropolis targets a sustained 27–28%, and printed 27.2%.
  • Dr Lal expects FY27 EBITDA "slightly better than last year".
  • Krsnaa: Rajasthan labs carry fixed costs ahead of utilisation. Channel-partner fees rose to 19% of revenue from 8%.
Network and M&A
  • Dr Lal closed S N GeneLab (FY26 revenue ~₹60 cr) and bought out Centrapath. It added Ghana and an Uzbekistan JV, and is hunting a South India target; the South is 6% of revenue.
  • Vijaya: three hubs and seven spokes planned for H2, and an entry into the North-East via Arya Wellness, Guwahati.
  • Krsnaa doubled its NABH radiology centres to 75.
  • Thyrocare added 1,180 franchisees, its most in three years, for 12,911+ active.
Portfolio clean-up
  • Thyrocare is divesting the Nueclear radiology business for ~₹141 cr. It took a ₹45 cr consolidated impairment. Pathology earns a 34.9% ROCE; Nueclear earned 7%.
  • Dr Lal's network has 312 labs and 7,727 patient service centres (FY26 count).
Specialty and wellness
  • Metropolis: specialty ₹198 cr (+20%), about 40% of revenue. TruHealth wellness ₹86 cr (+20%).
  • Dr Lal: SwasthFit bundles are 26% of revenue, flat YoY.
  • Vijaya: wellness 14.5% of revenue, +27%. Radiology is 37%.
  • Thyrocare is moving into reproductive genomics, NIPT and allergy panels.

Red flags

Ranked by how much they should change position sizing, not by how alarming they sound.

Act on these
Krsnaa
Revenue +32% became PAT +3%. Finance costs rose 89% to ₹15.5 cr. Channel-partner fees went from 8% to 19% of revenue. Tests rose 85% while revenue per patient fell. Government PPP growth is being bought with balance sheet and margin.
Sector-wide
The CGHS tariff revision accounts for 2–3 points of Q2 growth at the two highest-quality names. It annualises within a quarter. Any Q3 model that extrapolates Q2 growth is building on a one-time step-up.
Monitor next quarter
Thyrocare
Reported PAT fell 68% to ₹15 cr after a ₹45 cr impairment on the Nueclear divestment. The core pathology business is strong. But realisation per test is falling, since tests grew faster than revenue.
Metropolis
Its own release flags "heightened pricing competition in routine diagnostic test segments". Antique (5 Nov) holds a Hold rating on it, the only Hold among the brokers we found.
Suraksha
Cash was only ₹5.7 cr at quarter-end against ₹13.8 cr of H1 lease payments. It discloses no volume or EBITDA figures, so ours is derived.
Note and move on
Vijaya
Hyderabad is 67% of revenue. Without CGHS the margin is roughly the 42% guide, not 44%.
Dr Lal
SwasthFit's share is flat at 26%. GeneLab will consolidate fully from Q3, so the YoY comparison will include acquired growth.
Entero
It is a 5% EBITDA distributor in a diagnostics universe. Drop it from the sector, or score it against pharma distribution peers.

The call

Own now

Vijaya · Dr Lal

Vijaya has the highest margin, a 92% B2C mix, footfalls +14.5% and a 42%+ guide it can meet without CGHS. Dr Lal grew patients 9% and revenue per patient 9.7%, holds ₹1,746 cr of cash, and Antique raised its target after results. Both earn their growth rather than buy it.

Accumulate on evidence

Metropolis · Thyrocare · Suraksha

Metropolis is at the bottom of its 27–28% margin band, and competition is flagged. Wait for the price increase to show. Thyrocare's pathology core is excellent, but realisation per test is slipping; buy after the Nueclear exit closes. Suraksha needs a quarter of disclosed volumes.

Stay out

Krsnaa

The PPP model is growing tests 85% and earning 3% more profit. Interest, channel fees and Rajasthan's fixed costs absorb the growth. Revisit when finance costs stop rising and channel fees fall back toward 10% of revenue.

What would make us more positive
  • Q3 growth staying above 15% at Dr Lal and Vijaya after the CGHS step-up annualises.
  • A list-price increase at Metropolis or Vijaya that sticks without volume loss.
  • Krsnaa disclosing debtor days below 120 alongside Rajasthan utilisation.
What would make us cut
  • Volume growth falling below 8% at the incumbents, which would mean CGHS was masking a slowdown.
  • Aggregator or hospital-chain price competition reaching the B2C wellness bundles.
  • Another quarter of falling realisation at Thyrocare.

Caveats

  • All seven tracked companies have reported. Thyrocare was first, on 29 October. Suraksha was last, on 10 November.
  • Revenue per patient is not comparable across models. Vijaya reports per footfall and is 37% radiology. Krsnaa is mostly government PPP. Thyrocare is franchise-led B2B and discloses neither patients nor revenue per test.
  • Several figures are derived. Suraksha EBITDA (PBT + finance cost + D&A, including other income). Krsnaa revenue per patient (revenue ÷ patients). Thyrocare realisation (revenue growth − test growth). All are marked *.
  • Margin bases differ. Krsnaa's is before CSR and ESOP. Thyrocare's 33% is as reported on the call. Entero is a distributor.
  • The CGHS effect is quantified by only two companies. Metropolis, Thyrocare and Krsnaa did not say how much of their growth came from it.
  • Broker coverage is thin. Only Antique's notes on Dr Lal and Metropolis were visible. The consensus comparison uses a pre-season brokerage view (21 Sep), not a post-result poll.
  • Primary filings were not reachable during the build. Figures come from company releases and presentations as summarised by exchange-filing services, cross-checked across at least two sources where possible.
