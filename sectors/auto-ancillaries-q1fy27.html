What management said

From the Q1 FY27 (or equivalent) calls and releases. Grouped by theme, because the fifteen companies said broadly the same things in broadly the same order.

RM and wage pass-through lag (the dominant story)
  • Copper +40% YoY (Motherson); aluminium ₹260→₹360/kg mid-quarter (ZF CV), moderating into late July.
  • Nickel and aluminium, not steel, cited specifically by Sundram Fasteners — a different basket from the rest of the sector.
  • 30–40% NCR minimum-wage revision hit Motherson/MSWIL and Minda Corp's labour costs directly.
  • Recovery mechanism is near-universal: a 3–6 month contractual pass-through lag, cited almost verbatim across Motherson, MSWIL, Uno Minda, Endurance, Amara Raja, ZF CV and Minda Corp.
  • Ten of fifteen companies' EBITDA margin fell YoY on this mechanism; average margin change across the sector was a modest −38bps, but that average hides a genuinely bifurcated split — winners averaged +111bps, losers averaged −112bps.
EV and lithium — bifurcated bets, uneven returns
  • Exide's CEO: the current lithium pack-assembly business is “not long-term viable” without in-house cells; gigafactory commercialisation has slipped from “FY26-end” to “FY27, 25–30% utilisation.”
  • Amara Raja: lithium cells face a 15–20% cost disadvantage to Chinese competitors; the 16 GWh-by-FY30 target “may be revisited.”
  • Sona Comstar the clear exception — BEV at 44% of automotive revenue, an all-time high, plus two new DENSO electrification JVs.
  • Craftsman's Powertrain segment addresses EV risk by diversifying into non-automotive ICE (data-centre gensets) rather than an EV roadmap — explicitly unaddressed on the call.
  • No confirmed OEM purchase orders yet for either gigafactory (Exide, Amara Raja) — both remain at the homologation/customer-qualification stage.
Capex and the fundraise wave
  • Bharat Forge: ₹2,500cr fundraise approved alongside ₹1,800cr capex, weeks after a net-loss quarter.
  • Craftsman: ₹2,000cr QIP (priced mid-June) explicitly earmarked for deleveraging — despite the same call describing capex as “funded entirely from internal accruals.”
  • Motherson: ₹6,000cr FY27 capex plus Nexans and Yutaka Giken M&A closed this quarter, adding an estimated $2bn of annualised revenue from FY28.
  • Exide and Amara Raja gigafactory cumulative spend now ₹4,902cr and ₹1,650–1,900cr respectively.
Demand — share gains over pure OEM pass-through
  • India auto industry grew ~21.8% YoY in Q1 (2W +22.8%, PV +16.8%, 3W +39.1%) — management at Uno Minda, Minda Corp and Bosch each said they outpaced their underlying OEM segment.
  • e-2W registrations crossed 5 lakh units in a single quarter for the first time, cited by both Uno Minda and Minda Corp as validating content-per-vehicle investment.
  • Bosch's two-wheeler business grew 41.4% YoY on OBD-2 content and premiumisation — a regulatory tailwind, not a volume one.
Governance and leadership churn
  • CFO transitions mid-quarter or mid-process at Bosch (planned Group succession), ZF CV (a ~2-month gap spanning the results process) and Minda Corp (successor only now confirmed).
  • Craftsman's MD sold 2.01% of the company (~₹485.6cr) days after a dilutive QIP priced and weeks before a strong results beat — no reason disclosed.
  • Bharat Forge booked a second consecutive large exceptional/impairment charge (₹450cr Q4 FY26, ₹358cr Q1 FY27) — a pattern, not a one-off.
Exports — a mostly positive turn
  • Sundram Fasteners exports +23% YoY, reversing a full year of FY26 weakness (−8%), on nearly doubled North American Class 8 truck orders.
  • ZF CV exports +9.7% YoY, a modest positive turn after an −11.1% FY26.
  • Amara Raja the exception: automotive exports −20% YoY on Middle East weakness, even as SIAM reported record sector-wide export growth.
  • US tariff exposure (Class 8 trucks at Sundram Fasteners, North American EV OEMs at Sona Comstar) remains live and, in Sundram's case, unaddressed in the accessible transcript.

EBITDA margin guidance missed for a second consecutive, worsening quarter (13% target → 10.9% → 9.6%). New Energy remains a ₹22cr quarterly loss with no confirmed commercialisation timeline and a management-acknowledged 15–20% cost disadvantage to Chinese cells.

Uno Minda
Negative operating leverage — revenue +24–26% but PAT +2% — at the richest valuation in the sector (P/E ~58–59x, EV/EBITDA ~32x). Little room left for a repeat miss.

Motherson

Sundram Fasteners
Sell-side cut its 12-month target 15% (to ₹1,062) despite a clear 17–23% earnings beat — a valuation-quality signal, not an operating one.
Schaeffler
Reports on a calendar-year quarter (the company itself labels this period “Q2 2026”) — a comparability caveat against Indian-fiscal-year peers, not a flag on fundamentals.
Sector-wide

Auto ancillaries has no single rating-agency call the way cement has India Ratings — so this scorecard is built from the sector's own aggregated guidance: at the Q4 FY26 calls (April–May 2026), nearly every management team told the Street that RM and wage cost pass-through would compress Q1 margins, with recovery flagged for Q2 or H2. The call

Own now

Bosch · Sundram Fasteners

Clean, unambiguous beats with structural support, not one-offs. Bosch's headline PAT decline is a base-effect illusion — adjusted profit grew and margin expanded 71bps on genuine content and regulatory tailwinds. Sundram Fasteners delivered an export-led beat with modest margin dip and leverage under 1x — the sell-side target cut says more about multiple discomfort than about the operating quarter.

Buy the growth, watch the flag

Sona Comstar · Motherson · Craftsman · Minda Corp

Real growth stories where the near-term overhang is specific and known, not systemic: Sona Comstar's governance/US-EV softness, Motherson's MSWIL wage-and-copper lag, Craftsman's leverage-plus-insider-sale, Minda Corp's one-off-inflated headline PAT. None of these change the growth thesis by themselves — each is a reason to size the position with the flag in mind, not to avoid the name.

Stay out until the pattern breaks

Bharat Forge · Amara Raja · Motherson Sumi Wiring · Uno Minda

The margin miss here is now a pattern, not a one-quarter blip, and guidance credibility is wearing thin. Bharat Forge's standalone growth is badly lagging its own reiterated guidance and it just booked a net loss; Amara Raja has missed its margin target for two straight, worsening quarters; MSWIL's scissor and customer concentration compound each other; Uno Minda missed even its own already-lowered range at the richest multiple in the sector.

What would make us more positive
  • A Q2 print that actually shows the promised bps recovery at Uno Minda, Tube Investments or Amara Raja — not another quarter of the same guidance rolled forward.
  • Confirmed OEM purchase orders for either gigafactory (Exide, Amara Raja) — currently homologation-stage engagement only, despite ₹4,900cr+ and ₹1,650cr+ deployed respectively.
  • Aluminium and copper holding below their Q1 peaks (₹360/kg, +40% YoY) through Q2 and Q3.
What would make us cut
  • The Bharat Forge/Amara Raja pattern — a margin miss recurring for two-plus quarters — spreading to more names, evidence the lag has become structural rather than cyclical.
  • Craftsman: any further insider selling, or a disclosed post-QIP net debt/EBITDA still above 2.0x once the company reports it.
  • A US tariff or EV-policy escalation hitting the export-exposed names directly — Sundram Fasteners' Class 8 truck orders, Sona Comstar's North American EV OEM programmes.

Caveats

  • Reporting is complete. All fifteen companies in this universe have reported Q1 FY27 (or the equivalent calendar quarter). Granularity is thinner for the smaller, less-covered names — see the ZF CV Control Systems and Minda Corporation notes below.
  • Motherson's PAT figure carries a genuine source conflict. This report uses ₹1,075.7cr (+77.5% YoY, PAT attributable to owners, aligning with the PBT growth trend). A second set of sources (Upstox, Kotak Neo, citing the exchange filing) shows ₹1,032.1cr (+101.6% YoY) — possibly a different PAT line (e.g. pre-minority-interest). Verify against the primary BSE/NSE filing before acting on the precise figure.
  • Bharat Forge's EBITDA and margin are sourced from secondary/aggregator coverage, not a directly retrieved exchange filing or investor presentation. Sources disagree: EBITDA ₹710–752cr, margin 15.3–16.2%, contraction 120–190bps. This report uses the midpoint; treat the KPI-table figure as indicative, not precise.
  • Schaeffler India reports on a calendar-year quarter. The company itself labels the period covered here “Q2 2026” (Apr–Jun 2026) — the same calendar quarter as every Indian-fiscal-year peer's Q1 FY27, but the label mismatch is a real trap for anyone pulling figures from an aggregator without checking the period.
  • Standalone and consolidated bases diverge materially at two names. Exide's standalone margin (12.4%, +20bps) and consolidated margin (11.2%, −23bps) tell opposite stories — the EESL gigafactory is the difference. Endurance's management-quoted “RM-lag-adjusted” standalone margin (13.33%) is not the same figure as either its reported standalone (11.2%) or consolidated (13.1%) margin — this report uses consolidated throughout the KPI table and league table.
  • Several PAT figures are inflated or deflated by one-off items. Bharat Forge's net loss reflects a ₹358cr Germany restructuring charge; Bosch's −37.1% headline PAT reflects a Q1 FY26 exceptional gain in the base, not a Q1 FY27 weakness (adjusted PAT +9.9%); Minda Corporation's +215.8% headline PAT includes a ₹106cr non-cash Minda VAST consolidation gain (adjusted growth closer to +53%); ZF CV Control Systems' −14.6% PAT decline is largely a high base effect from a one-off forex gain a year ago (adjusted PBT ex-forex +16.9%).
  • Tube Investments has two PAT lines that are easy to conflate. Total consolidated PAT before minority interest was ₹294cr (−3% YoY); PAT attributable to TI's own shareholders, after the CG Power minority interest, was ₹168.6cr (−15% YoY). This report uses the latter, correct, basis throughout.
  • Uno Minda's own investor materials show a “normalized” PAT and margin that differ from the as-reported GAAP figures used in this report (normalized PAT +24% vs reported +2.1%; normalized margin -40bps vs reported -181bps). The as-reported figures are used here as the better-corroborated basis.
  • Coverage is thin for ZF CV Control Systems and Minda Corporation. Both are less-covered by sell-side and aggregator sources than the larger names in this universe; several figures (net debt/EBITDA, precise EBITDA) could not be independently corroborated across multiple sources and should be cross-checked against the primary filing before use.
  • Net debt/EBITDA is undisclosed on a quarterly basis for the majority of names. Marked “n/d” throughout rather than estimated; several “net cash” characterisations rely on FY26 year-end balance-sheet data, the most recent available, rather than a Q1 FY27-specific figure.
  • Forward statements are projections, not outcomes. FY27 capex guidance, margin-recovery timelines, gigafactory commissioning dates and Vision-2030-style long-run targets are all management or analyst views, not delivered results.
