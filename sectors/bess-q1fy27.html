The scissor

The 3 GWh+ BESS pure plays (ACME) and the storage-rich grid OEMs (Hitachi) are the only two where revenue and margin moved the same direction by a wide margin.

What management said

From the Q1 FY27 calls and releases. Grouped by theme, because the companies said broadly the same things.

Order book and BESS pipeline
  • Waaree Energies: ₹61,500 Cr record order book, ₹16,000 Cr added in Q1 alone. Cell-to-module integration target lifted from 20% to 65% over two quarters.
  • Hitachi Energy: first BESS order — 165 MW / 330 MWh in Andhra Pradesh. Order backlog at all-time high of ₹32,222 Cr.
  • JSW Energy: first external BESS order of ₹440 Cr; 5 GWh plant already commissioned in Q4 FY26. Margin guide $2.75–3/kWh.
  • Premier Energies: ₹15,000 Cr order book, 100% domestic, DCR module capacity sold out through FY28.
  • Genus Power: ₹24,020 Cr executable order book, 92% tied to JV/platform with GIC.
  • ACME Solar: ₹1,400+ Cr of BESS revenue locked in for FY27 at ₹8–10/unit.
  • Waaree Renewable: ₹5,300 Cr order book, including a 1,520 MWh BESS project; tracking 27 GW domestic + 10 GW international pipeline.
Cost and margin — the second story
  • Waaree Energies: EBITDA margin fell to 18.2% from 22.5% YoY — raw material costs and China metal price moves cited.
  • Premier Energies: held 30.3% margin via operational leverage and backward integration. Non-DCR module business explicitly "not profitable" right now.
  • ACME Solar: 87.1% overall margin; core renewable at ~91%, BESS at ~82% (lower because charging power passes through opex).
  • Amara Raja: standalone margin held at 10.1%; consolidated compressed to 9.6% on brand spending, Amaron Assist, and strategic investments.
  • HBL Engineering: EBITDA margin dropped to 23% from 31.9% YoY — employee cost inflation and defence battery revenue halved.
  • KPI Green: revenue up 16%, EBITDA up 21%, but PAT fell 14% on depreciation and finance costs from new IPP commissioning.
Capacity buildout
  • Waaree Energies: 24.2 GW module, 5.4 GW cell, target to add 10 GW cell by end-FY27.
  • Premier Energies: 5.6 GW Seetharampur module plant operational; 7 GW TOPCon cell line trial runs August 2026.
  • Exide Industries: 4 production lines at Bengaluru gigafactory live, utilities 100% operational, lithium sample dispatches underway.
  • Amara Raja: ₹1,700 Cr FY27 capex (₹1,300 Cr new energy); Telangana giga corridor Customer Qualification Plant commissioned July 2026.
  • Hitachi Energy: construction of 20th manufacturing unit at Karjan, Vadodara — ₹1,600–2,000 Cr investment, 30,000 MVA capacity.
  • ACME Solar: ~3.62 GWh cumulative BESS commissioned; raised FY27 target to 10+ GWh.
Demand and policy
  • Sector-wide: FDRE and hybrid auctions dominated Q1 FY27. SECI FDRE-IX tender (1,200 MW + 4,800 MWh BESS) launched mid-June 2026.
  • Industry estimate: ~250 GW of module manufacturing capacity vs ~60 GW annual demand — non-DCR oversupply flagged by Premier, Waaree, and ACME.
  • Genus Power: government target of 31–32 Cr smart meters to transition; ~8–9 Cr expected to be tendered in FY27.
  • Premier Energies: ALMM-II extension to Dec 2026 drove a near-term non-DCR order surge.
  • JSW Energy: Q1 national power demand growth +8.5%; Q1 BESS external order the first sign of revenue scale.
Battery storage economics
  • JSW Energy BESS guide: ~₹150 Cr annual EBITDA at full capacity, $2.75–3/kWh.
  • ACME Solar BESS: >20% annual EBITDA-to-capex yield; 10 GWh/year expected for merchant market over 5 years.
  • Amara Raja BESS operating margins guided at a thin 5–8% — vs 91% core renewable margin at ACME.
  • Sterling & Wilson Egypt BESS+Solar: $560M, 1,000 MW solar + 600 MWh BESS, third gigawatt-scale order in nine months.
  • Tata Power: rooftop battery bundled product launched ("MySine") — 1.5–2x revenue per customer vs plain rooftop.
Strategic shifts
  • Waaree Energies: 5.15 GWh BESS container facility at Rola operational via Waaree ESS subsidiary. Inverter duty transformers commercialised. US module plant 1.6 GW at 59% utilisation.
  • Waaree Renewable: 55% stake in Associated Power Structures (T&D EPC) acquired for ₹243 Cr+ — entry into grid infrastructure.
  • Premier Energies: Transcon transformer stake now contributing ₹110 Cr quarterly; 10 GVA HV/EHV expansion by Sept 2026.
  • ACME Solar: 600 MW of FDRE/hybrid PPAs signed with SECI; bids won for short-term BESS contracts (DEEP portal, HP-TAM markets).
  • KPI Green: 565 MW / 1,130 MWh BESPA executed for two projects; 5 GW MOU with Botswana (revenue FY28+).

Ranked by how much they should change position sizing, not by how alarming they sound.

JSW Energy
PAT fell 36% YoY despite stable revenue. The cost is interest (₹1,519 Cr) and depreciation (₹890 Cr, +20% YoY) from commissioned capacity. Net debt ₹55,000 Cr at 4.95x EBITDA — a typical build-phase pattern, but the EBITDA has to deliver on the additions.
Waaree Energies
EBITDA margin compressed 430bps YoY (22.5% → 18.2%) — raw material costs and China metal price moves. Order book 36% US-bound; AD/CVD determination on US imports adverse in June 2026. Legal remedies under evaluation.
Amara Raja
Standalone EBITDA margin held at 10.1%, but consolidated at 9.6% — the gap is the new energy business. BESS operating margins guided at 5–8%, well below consolidated average. Automotive international rev down 20% on Middle East geopolitics.
KPI Green
PAT down 14% YoY despite +16% revenue growth. Interest cost up 111%, depreciation up 70% on new IPP commissioning. Cash PAT still grew 6% — but reported earnings will look weak for 2–3 more quarters as the IPP base scales.
Note and move on
Waaree Renewable
Standalone solar EPC margin near 19% but consolidated falls to ~15% after 12 days of APSPL T&D consolidation. The T&D acquisition is strategically sound, but 75% debt-funded — leverage is rising on day one.
Premier Energies
Order book 100% domestic and DCR-led. If ALMM-II does not get a further extension and the non-DCR window closes, Q2 FY27 may be weaker than Q1.
Sector-wide
"BESS is a theme, not a sector" — the segment disclosure is poor across the board. ACME is the only name that reports BESS revenue separately. Hitachi's first BESS order is one project, not a series. The rest is capacity that is being built, not revenue that is being booked.

The call

Own now

ACME Solar · Hitachi Energy

ACME is the only name reporting BESS as a separate revenue line, with 87% overall margin, 3.62 GWh commissioned, and FY27 BESS revenue already 40% locked in. Hitachi's first BESS order lands it on a sector that has very few listed entries; the ₹32,222 Cr order backlog (record) underwrites the next 6–8 quarters of execution. Both have transparency on segment economics — rare in this universe.

Accumulate into Q2 weakness

Premier Energies · Exide · Waaree RTL · Tata Power (TP Solar)

All four are in heavy build phase. Premier held 30% margins through the cycle and has FY28 DCR order book sold out. Exide is on plan for Bengaluru gigafactory revenue in FY27. Waaree RTL has 27 GW of pipeline it is converting. Tata Power's TP Solar grew PAT 3.9x — but the parent group has too many moving parts to underwrite segment numbers. Buy the build-out, not the headline.

Stay out

High Energy Batteries · HBL Engineering · Sterling & Wilson

High Energy is loss-making with no segment disclosure. HBL's Kavach win is real but the 9pp margin compression and defence battery halving is not a one-quarter blip. Sterling & Wilson is the only name with negative revenue growth in the universe, and the working capital is negative — a tell that the project mix is shifting in ways that do not show in the P&L yet.

What would make us more positive
  • Any name reporting BESS segment economics — revenue and margin separately from solar/EPC. ACME today, Hitachi starting, others later. Once 2–3 names start, the sector becomes investable on a real basis.
  • SECI's 4,800 MWh FDRE-IX award converting into firm orders. Watch for the winners list in Q2 FY27 results.
  • Waaree Energies EBITDA margin recovering to >20% in Q2 — would mean the China metal input shock has passed and the order book is being executed at full margin.
What would make us cut
  • Lead and polysilicon prices staying elevated through Q2 — would extend the margin compression for Exide, Amara Raja, Waaree.
  • SECI or state tenders undersubscribed, or tariffs above ₹6.50/kWh — would mean the BESS economics have broken before the capex lands.
  • Sterling & Wilson customer counter-claim outcome adverse — would remove a key solvency argument.

Caveats

  • BESS is a theme, not a sector. No listed Indian pure play exists at scale. Every name in the universe is a battery, IPP, solar or grid-equipment business with storage attached. Read the quarter for order-book and commentary disclosure, not for segment numbers that mostly do not exist. This report is intentionally honest about that — ACME is the only name with a BESS-segment EBITDA disclosure.
  • Returns table is blank by design. Kite was not available. Cells marked n/s have not been substituted with aggregator data, which is known to disagree with the exchange feed.
  • Standalone vs consolidated bases differ. Most visible at Amara Raja (consolidated margin 9.6% vs standalone 10.1%) and HBL Engineering (consolidated margin 23% includes defence battery, Kavach and rail signalling). Comparing like with like requires the segment disclosure that most names do not provide.
  • Capacity buildout ≠ revenue. Waaree Energies has 5.4 GW of cell capacity and a 5.15 GWh BESS plant, but its revenue is overwhelmingly solar modules. Premier has 12 GWh of BESS under construction but the FY27 revenue contribution is small. JSW Energy has a 5 GWh BESS plant and just booked its first external order.
  • Non-DCR module economics are not the BESS story. Premier, Waaree, and ACME all flag ~250 GW of module capacity chasing 60 GW of demand. Non-DCR pricing is "not profitable" in Premier's own words. The DCR policy regime is the real tailwind, and the smart-metering and BESS narratives are secondary.
  • Beat/miss is judgment-based. Broker previews and consensus numbers for most of this universe are thin. The walk-the-talk section is grounded in management guidance from the Q4 FY26 calls where it was available, and in stated FY27 targets from the investor presentations.
  • One-off items and accounting. ACME's 87% margin includes the BESS revenue and the cost of power purchased for charging in opex — segment margin is closer to 82% on the BESS portion alone. ACME's core renewable is 91%.
  • Order book composition varies. Waaree Energies' ₹61,500 Cr is mostly solar modules with 90% from solar. ACME's ₹1,400 Cr BESS revenue lock-in is the only BESS-specific contract disclosure in the universe. Hitachi's ₹32,222 Cr is mostly transformers and grid, with BESS a single 165 MW / 330 MWh order.
  • Forward statements are projections, not outcomes. Q2 cost guidance, FY27 BESS commissioning targets, and tariff levels are all management or analyst views.
