What management said

From the Q2 FY27 calls, releases and presentations. Grouped by theme.

Drug and consumable pricing
  • Max: the hospital pricing of drugs, devices and consumables is sub judice. The sector may need to "recalibrate" to services-based pricing.
  • Fortis has not quantified the cap's impact. Drugs are about a third of its oncology revenue.
  • HCG expects a mid-to-high single-digit hit for one or two quarters, and nothing material from FY28.
  • Rainbow put the impact below 0.5 points of margin a quarter (PL, 3 Nov).
Payor mix and price
  • Max: institutional bed share fell to 29% from 34%. BLK left CGHS and Railways over non-payment. CGHS revisions added a net ~₹200 cr a year, against ~₹100 cr lost on discontinued chemo drugs.
  • Fortis: ARPP +6.8%, of which price was only about 1.5 points. The rest was case mix.
  • HCG: non-institutional share reached 69%, with that revenue up 21%.
  • Rainbow guides ARPOB growth of 6–8%. It delivered about 11–15%.
New capacity
  • Max: brownfield beds break even within a quarter, and Max Smart did it in three months. The Gurgaon greenfield opens from end-FY27, with an EBITDA loss of ₹40–50 cr.
  • Medanta: Noida turned EBITDA positive at ₹8.6 cr. The company plans about 3,450 more beds for over ₹6,000 cr.
  • KIMS: Kondapur margin back to ~30% in 3–4 quarters; Andhra oncology costs to normalise in 2–3.
  • Jupiter: Dombivli's loss fell to ₹0.2 cr from ₹9.5 cr in Q1.
Guidance changes
  • Fortis declined to give FY28 margin guidance, citing drug-price noise. FY27 hospital margin is "more or less intact".
  • Rainbow lowered revenue growth to the high teens from ~20% CAGR.
  • Max expects revenue growth to accelerate in Q3 and Q4 as the chemo base effect fades.
  • HCG: mid-teens revenue growth and high-teens to ~20% EBITDA growth.
Balance sheets and capital
  • Max: Q2 free cash flow of ₹500 cr. Net debt ₹2,369 cr, under 1x EBITDA.
  • Fortis: IHH wants to go above 50%. The CFO says that could bring about ₹10,000 cr over time.
  • Yatharth: Advent's ₹3,150 cr investment for 24.9% awaits approvals.
  • Artemis: a ~₹700 cr fund raise has shareholder approval.
International
  • Narayana: Cayman insurance losses fell to $1.1 mn from $3.7 mn in Q1. The UK (Practice Plus) earned a 7.3% margin, down 150 bps QoQ.
  • Max international revenue was ₹307 cr, +33%. Apollo's grew 37%.
  • HCG's Kenya revenue grew about 54%.
  • Aster realised ₹16.6 cr of merger synergies against a ₹200 cr full potential.

Red flags

Ranked by how much they should change position sizing, not by how alarming they sound.

Act on these
HCG
It is the most exposed chain to the cancer-drug cap and guides a mid-to-high single-digit hit. Reported PAT fell 71% to ₹4.8 cr after a ₹11.6 cr EPCG provision. Adjusted PAT missed PL by 40%. Net debt rose to ₹490 cr from ₹340 cr in March. Cash conversion fell to about 54% of EBITDA from 80%+.
Fortis
It withheld FY28 margin guidance, and the 25% target sat at the centre of its story. A court-ordered forensic audit is open, with no completion date. Its ARPOB disclosure jumped 21% while ARPP rose 6.8%, so the series is not usable until the basis is explained.
Narayana
Revenue +73% is mostly the UK acquisition. The UK earns a 7.3% margin and a 4–5% ROCE, and carries about ₹1,710 cr of the group's ₹1,883 cr net debt. Collections from Indian government payors and Cayman insurers slowed in H1.
Monitor next quarter
KIMS
Revenue +40%, yet the margin is down 120 bps YoY and H1 PAT fell 28%. It took an ₹8 cr doubtful-debt provision on government business. The Kondapur margin recovery is now 3–4 quarters out.
Rainbow
It cut revenue guidance to high teens. Occupancy fell 280 bps YoY to 49.2%. Margin and PAT came in slightly below PL, and PL cut its target to ₹1,650 from ₹1,900.
Max
Oncology's share of IP revenue fell to 21% from 26%, and chemo is about 30% of oncology. That exposure now meets the trade-margin cap. PAT +10% trails EBITDA +18%.
Apollo
It discloses no ARPOB. New hospitals lost ₹35 cr of EBITDA. Reported PAT includes discontinued operations from the October stake transfers.
Note and move on
Yatharth
Finance cost rose to ₹8.3 cr from ₹0.3 cr and D&A rose 75%, so PAT grew 17% on revenue up 52%. The Advent deal is still pending approval.
Artemis
The Raipur unit cost ₹8.6 cr of EBITDA (implied). A ~₹700 cr fund raise means dilution ahead of the South Delhi project.
Shalby
Standalone margin fell 130 bps and operational beds fell 3.5%. Consolidated margin is just 13.2%, and guided tax is 45–50%.
Sector-wide
Hospital pricing of drugs and consumables is before the Supreme Court. A 16% margin cap was floated there before the 30% cap was set.

The call

Own now

Medanta · Max

Medanta is the cleanest print in the group: margin +420 bps, Noida EBITDA-positive, EBITDA 23% above PL's estimate, and a net-cash balance sheet funding the next 3,450 beds. Max added 14% to its beds without losing occupancy or margin and generated ₹500 cr of free cash in the quarter. Max is the more cap-exposed of the two, so size it smaller.

Accumulate on evidence

Apollo · Narayana · Aster · Jupiter

All four executed. Each has one open question. Apollo: what its new hospitals lose once Gurugram opens in Q3. Narayana: whether the UK margin turns up from 7.3%. Aster: whether synergies scale from ₹16.6 cr toward the ₹65 cr runway. Jupiter: whether Dombivli stays at breakeven while Pune is built on debt.

Stay out

HCG · Fortis · Shalby

HCG takes the drug cap in full while its net debt rises and cash conversion halves. Fortis has withdrawn its margin roadmap and still has a forensic audit open. Shalby's consolidated margin of 13% leaves nothing to underwrite. Revisit Fortis when the audit closes or IHH's stake increase is priced.

What would make us more positive
  • Q3 calls putting the cap at under 1% of EBITDA for Max, Apollo and Fortis.
  • The Supreme Court leaving hospital consumable pricing to the 30% framework, not the 16% floated earlier.
  • Gurugram (Apollo) and Gurgaon Sector-56 (Max) opening within the losses guided, about ₹40–50 cr for Max.
What would make us cut
  • The trade-margin cap extending from oncology to consumables, implants or all non-scheduled drugs.
  • Another quarter of government and insurer receivables stretching, as Narayana and KIMS flagged.
  • Max's institutional exits pulling occupancy below 75%.

Caveats

  • All 13 tracked chains have reported. Jupiter was first, on 23 October. Aster was last, on 6 November.
  • ARPOB is not a common currency. Seven chains disclose it. Apollo, Max and Medanta report revenue per inpatient (ARPP) instead. HCG's figure is ARPP per day. Artemis covers the Gurgaon hospital only. Shalby is standalone. Jupiter's is an H1 figure.
  • The Fortis ARPOB series looks rebased. Axis (9 Nov) reports ₹83,369 against ₹68,767 a year earlier (+21%), while the company's ARPP grew 6.8%. We show the figure and score nothing on it.
  • Margin bases differ. Apollo is the hospital segment. Fortis is pre-ESOP. Narayana is the India hospital business; its consolidated margin is 21.3% in its release and 19.5% in PL's note. Rainbow is post-Ind AS. Shalby is standalone. Do not rank across bases closer than about two points.
  • Some figures are derived, not reported. Bed growth for Apollo, Medanta, Aster and Yatharth is computed from reported bed counts. Ramp-up drag for Apollo, HCG and Artemis is our arithmetic from disclosed losses over revenue. They are marked * where shown.
  • Acquisitions distort YoY. Narayana's +73% is the UK. Aster's growth is against a pro-forma merged base. HCG's base excludes the divested fertility business.
  • Source conflicts are flagged, not resolved. Narayana EBITDA (₹605 cr vs ₹554 cr); HCG revenue growth (15.8% vs 13.1%); Medanta beds (3,115 operational vs ~3,811 total in PL).
  • Broker coverage is what was publicly visible. No post-result note was found for Aster, Yatharth, Shalby or Artemis. Several result notes surfaced only through aggregators.
  • Primary filings were not reachable for every name. Max's figures are confirmed against its earnings-call transcript (filed 9 Nov). For the rest we relied on releases and presentations as summarised by exchange-filing services and on broker notes, because the filings service was down during the build.
