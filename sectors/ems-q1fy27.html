Sector earnings review · Quarter ended 30 June 2026

Electronics Manufacturing Services
Q1 FY27

All ten companies here grew revenue by double digits this quarter — the growth story is not in dispute. What is in dispute is the quality behind it: PLI income vanished, was reversed, or went undisclosed at half the sample; two companies carried a qualified audit opinion; two lost a plant to fire, one with fatalities. Strip out the incentive income and the one-offs, and the dispersion in this sector is a margin-and-governance story, not a growth one.

EBITDA margin — the league table

The sector's core unit economic, structurally thin at 3–8% for most names here. Kaynes' 15.6% and Elin's 1.1% sit at opposite ends of the same industry — content mix (turnkey box-build and precision electronics vs. commoditised lighting and small appliances) explains more of the gap than scale does. Dixon's figure is its adjusted, company-wide margin (Mobile & EMS segment alone is lower, at 2.6%); Syrma's two disclosed figures for the same quarter (10.2% narrative vs. a 99bps-implying variance table) are flagged in the caveats.

What management said

From the Q1 FY27 calls and releases. Grouped by theme, because most of the ten companies are telling variations of the same three or four stories.

Component and metal cost inflation
  • Kaynes quantified it precisely: copper +40%, aluminium +46%, silver +149%, PCB prices “tripled” YoY — the most specific input-cost disclosure in the sector this quarter.
  • Dixon's RM cost rose to 94.2% of revenue (from 92.5%); PG Electroplast's to 85.5% (from 84.1%); Elin's to 78.4% (from 72.0%), the last compounded by a 25% Ghaziabad wage hike.
  • Only 50–70% of RM and labour cost increases have been passed through so far at Elin — a partial, lagging recovery mechanism echoed, less precisely, at PGEL and Dixon.
  • RM cost at Kaynes grew +57% YoY, outpacing 40% revenue growth — the clearest single line explaining its margin and PAT miss.
PLI income — volatile, opaque, and now a real earnings risk
  • Dixon's Mobile PLI 1.0 formally expired at FY26-end — the single largest driver of its margin step-down, with no confirmed successor income and recovery pointed only to FY28 (PLI 2.0).
  • PG Electroplast and EPACK Durable both stated zero PLI income this quarter; EPACK's was ₹13.3cr a year ago, and FY26 already saw a ₹32.42cr PLI reversal for missing a growth threshold — a twice-materialised, binary risk.
  • Syrma's PLI quantum for the quarter could not be found in sources reviewed — likely embedded, undisclosed, in the gap between its operating and total EBITDA margin.
  • Amber has ECMS scheme approvals in its capex pipeline rather than PLI income in its P&L this quarter; Centum's ₹106cr ECMS approval (transducers and filters) landed after the quarter closed.
  • Five of ten companies carried an explicit PLI-linked margin or earnings swing this quarter — the single most repeated distorting factor in this report's KPI table.
Growth funded by working capital
  • Kaynes' net working-capital days jumped 125→163 in one quarter, the sharpest move in the sector, with 96% of the receivables build traced to smart-metering alone.
  • Syrma's WC days rose 37→68 annually and kept deteriorating sequentially to 71 within Q1, funded by stretched payables and fresh short-term borrowing.
  • Centum's WC days moved from -6 (self-funding) to 49 over two years; Dixon's operating cash flow was negative ₹341cr despite a headline PAT beat, on ₹812cr of working-capital absorption.
  • Cyient DLM's free cash flow was negative ₹17.1cr, management citing higher inventory and reduced customer advances directly.
OSAT and semiconductor ambition — early, and monetising slowly
  • Kaynes' Sanand OSAT project and Cyient DLM's semiconductor-equipment opportunity are both still pre-revenue or early-stage; Cyient DLM explicitly said no incremental capex is planned even for this opportunity, calling itself asset-light.
  • Centum's semiconductor-equipment sub-segment scaled from near-zero in FY25 to over ₹100cr in FY26, guided to $25–30mn within 1–2 years at 10–11% margin.
  • Avalon's newly announced ₹1,000cr Zollner Elektronik JV (post-quarter) targets multi-vertical EMS — healthcare, life sciences, test & measurement, rail — not specifically semiconductor equipment, a broader ambition than the Q1 call suggested.
Fire, safety and audit qualifications
  • A fire at Amber's IL JIN Electronics subsidiary (4 Aug 2026) killed two local fire-department personnel; a separate fire at Elin's Ghaziabad plant (25 May 2026) triggered a ₹24.6cr exceptional provision with no insurance recovery yet recognised.
  • Two of ten companies carry a qualified FY26 audit opinion: Amber (S.R. Batliboi, a recurring pattern also seen in FY23) and EPACK Durable (Deloitte, over a disputed ₹19.61cr receivable) — both auditors were subsequently reappointed.
  • EPACK's promoter declared no share pledge for FY26, a positive directly answering the standard governance check.
Customer concentration and mix shift
  • EPACK's top-2 customer concentration improved from 72% (FY23) to 38% — a genuine diversification story, though still elevated.
  • Syrma disclosed a full concentration ladder: top-5 38%, top-10 51%, top-20 66%; ODM share rising to 17% (from 12%), targeting ~25%.
  • Avalon's mix shift toward Clean Energy (29% of revenue, from 19%, +141% YoY) and box-build (56%, from 44%) is the clearest margin-accretive mix story in the sector this quarter.

Red flags

Ranked by how much they should change position sizing, not by how alarming they sound.

Act on these
EPACK Durable
Deloitte qualified the FY26 audit opinion over a disputed ₹19.61cr trade receivable of uncertain recoverability — then was reappointed for a second consecutive five-year term three weeks later. Layer on a twice-materialised PLI risk (a ₹32.42cr FY26 reversal plus zero PLI income this quarter) and the earnings quality here needs independent verification before sizing.
Elin Electronics
Core operations were loss-making even before the Ghaziabad fire (pre-exceptional PBT -₹3.8cr vs +₹12.7cr YoY). Net cash fell from ₹70.1cr to ₹5.9cr YoY, FY27 revenue guidance was roughly halved, and the standing margin target was explicitly withdrawn as “unlikely as of now.”
Amber Enterprises
A recurring qualified audit opinion (S.R. Batliboi, also flagged FY23) over unaudited subsidiary/JV financials, alongside a fire at the IL JIN subsidiary that killed two fire-department personnel. Net debt near-doubled QoQ to ₹1,225cr against ~₹4,700cr of committed capex and FY26 EBITDA of only ₹970cr.
Monitor next quarter
Kaynes Technology
NWC days jumped 125→163 in a single quarter, the sector's sharpest deterioration, alongside an unexplained tax-rate jump (~22-23%→~36%) that drove PAT down 24.4% despite a PBT decline of only 8.5%. Guidance was itself stepped back from a specific number to a qualitative one.
Centum Electronics
~90% of headline PAT is a one-off deconsolidation gain; recurring PAT was only about ₹11cr. Three-year average ROE is negative, working capital days swung from -6 to 49, and management itself called the quarter “muted” against its own FY27 guidance.
Syrma SGS
Working capital days rose from 37 to 71 in five quarters, funded by stretched payables and fresh short-term debt, alongside a mid-quarter CEO transition. None of this is disqualifying on its own, but the combination bears watching against a very rich growth-led valuation.
Dixon Technologies
Operating cash flow was negative ₹341cr despite a headline PAT beat that was ~75% a non-recurring fair-value gain; core/adjusted PAT actually fell 3% YoY. The Vivo JV closing remains under an ED money-laundering probe.
Note and move on
Cyient DLM
Coverage is thin: net debt/EBITDA figures disagree materially across sources (1.22x vs 2.40x), unreconciled in this pass. A strong YoY headline masks real QoQ softening (margin -117bps, PAT -27.4%) and negative free cash flow.
PG Electroplast
Whether the year-ago 9.3% base-quarter margin itself contained PLI benefit could not be confirmed — the open question behind the “core margin actually up” thesis this report leans on.
Avalon Technologies
PAT fell 15.3% QoQ with no clear explanation in sources reviewed, at the richest valuation in the sector (P/E ~91x, EV/EBITDA ~61x) — little room for a repeat miss.
Sector-wide
PLI disclosure is inconsistent across the sample — some companies quantify it precisely, several don't disclose it at all. Treat any margin comparison across this table as partly a comparison of disclosure quality, not just operating performance.

Did the sector's own guidance hold?

EMS has no single rating-agency benchmark call the way cement has India Ratings — so this scorecard is built from the sector's own aggregated guidance at the FY26 exit calls (April–June 2026): revenue growth would stay strong, margin would hold or improve as PLI-era capacity matured, and working capital would normalise as scale built. Here is the scorecard against what ten companies actually delivered.

Right
Revenue growth would stay strong. All ten companies grew revenue at least 13% YoY, several above 40% — the growth half of the FY26 exit-call story held completely.
Wrong
That margin would hold or improve as PLI-era capacity matured. It didn't, for six of ten — PLI 1.0-era incentive income is expiring, being reversed, or simply not being disclosed faster than the capacity it funded is generating offsetting scale economics.
Partly
“Working capital would normalise as scale built.” The opposite happened at three of the sector's fastest growers — Kaynes, Syrma and Centum all saw working-capital days deteriorate sharply alongside their strongest revenue growth, not despite it.
Wrong
That cost pressure alone would explain the dispersion. It doesn't: Avalon and Cyient DLM expanded margin through the same RM-inflation environment everyone else cited as the reason for missing. Mix (box-build, Clean Energy, Aerospace) and disclosure quality mattered more than the shared input-cost story.
Wrong
That the sector's PLI transition would be an orderly, disclosed process. It has instead produced two reversals (EPACK), one outright expiry with no confirmed successor (Dixon), and at least one company (Syrma) where the quantum simply cannot be found in public sources.

The call

Own now

Avalon Technologies · PG Electroplast

The two clean, structurally-supported beats. Avalon's margin expansion is mix-driven (Clean Energy, box-build), not incentive-driven, even if the valuation leaves little room for a stumble. PGEL's margin miss is fully explained by RM inflation with management stating explicitly there was zero PLI content this quarter — and its own ex-PLI 8% target was reaffirmed, not walked back.

Buy the growth, watch the flag

Kaynes Technology · Dixon Technologies · Syrma SGS · Amber Enterprises

Real, well-documented growth, each carrying a specific, known overhang rather than a systemic one: Kaynes' working-capital deterioration and stepped-back guidance, Dixon's negative operating cash flow behind a one-off-inflated headline, Syrma's working-capital stretch alongside a CEO transition, Amber's recurring audit qualification and fire. None of these overturn the growth thesis alone — each is a reason to size the position with the flag in mind.

Stay out until the pattern breaks

Elin Electronics · Centum Electronics · EPACK Durable · Cyient DLM

Elin's core business was loss-making before its fire, with both growth and margin guidance now cut; Centum's headline profit is ~90% non-recurring against a negative three-year ROE and a "muted" quarter by management's own words; EPACK carries a twice-proven, threshold-dependent PLI risk plus an unresolved audit qualification; Cyient DLM's strong YoY headline sits atop real QoQ softening and negative free cash flow, at a rich valuation, with thin independent coverage.

What would make us more positive
  • A Q2 print showing PLI clarity — Dixon confirming replacement incentive income, or Syrma finally quantifying its PLI quantum — rather than another quarter of the same disclosure gap.
  • Working-capital days stabilising or reversing at Kaynes, Syrma and Centum, evidence the growth-funded-by-WC pattern was a one-quarter scale effect, not a structural shift.
  • Component costs (copper, aluminium, silver, PCB) holding below their Q1 peaks through Q2 and Q3, giving the RM-inflation-hit names room to recover margin.
What would make us cut
  • A third PLI reversal or threshold miss anywhere in the sector, evidence the incentive-income risk is systemic rather than company-specific.
  • Another fire or safety incident at any of these facilities — two in one quarter is already a pattern worth watching, not an isolated tail event.
  • Either qualified-audit company (Amber, EPACK) drawing a further qualification or a going-concern-adjacent note at the next audit cycle.

Caveats

  • Reporting is complete. All ten companies in this universe have reported Q1 FY27. Coverage depth varies considerably — Cyient DLM and Centum Electronics are both thinly covered by sell-side and aggregator sources relative to the larger names, and several requested data points (customer concentration, exact capex, precise leverage ratios) were genuinely unavailable for both within this pass.
  • Syrma's EBITDA margin YoY change carries an unresolved source conflict. One broker's own report states both "+13 bps YoY" in narrative text and a variance table implying roughly +99bps for the same comparison. This report uses the narrative figure (+13bps) as the more precise, but the disagreement could not be reconciled within this pass; verify against the primary exchange filing before acting on the precise figure.
  • Centum's revenue-growth direction required active correction. One aggregator (Univest) showed a 25.33% YoY revenue decline, traced to a comparison against the wrong prior-period quarter; the correct comparison shows growth of 11-14% depending on standalone/consolidated basis. This report uses the corrected, growth figure throughout.
  • PAT figures are inflated or deflated by one-off items at half the sample. Dixon's headline PAT is ~75% a non-recurring fair-value gain (adjusted PAT -3% YoY); Centum's is ~90% a one-off deconsolidation gain (recurring PAT ~₹11cr); Amber's exceptional Ascent Circuits charge nearly wiped out headline profit (adjusted PAT +19%); Elin swung to a net loss even before its fire-related exceptional provision.
  • Amber's reported PAT figure is disputed and unresolved across sources (₹3.1cr vs ₹22.3cr) — likely a minority-interest or line-item difference that could not be reconciled within this pass. The adjusted/underlying PAT figure (+19% YoY) is better corroborated and used throughout this report's tiles and KPI table.
  • Net debt/EBITDA and leverage figures are inconsistent or undisclosed for most of the sample. Cyient DLM's own coverage disagrees materially (1.22x vs 2.40x); Amber, Kaynes, Syrma, EPACK and Dixon do not disclose a quarterly net debt/EBITDA figure in sources reviewed. This report substitutes working-capital or inventory days as the sector's more meaningful leverage constraint, per the metric map for this sector, rather than estimating a net debt figure.
  • Working-capital and inventory-day figures are not on a consistent basis. Some companies disclose net working-capital (NWC) days, others only inventory days (DIO); the KPI table notes which basis applies to each row. None should be compared directly across that basis divide.
  • PLI income is inconsistently disclosed sector-wide. Dixon, PGEL and EPACK quantify or characterise it explicitly; Syrma's Q1 PLI quantum could not be found in any source reviewed; Amber and Centum's PLI-adjacent disclosures (ECMS approvals) are pipeline items, not P&L income, and are not comparable to the others in this table.
  • Forward statements are projections, not outcomes. FY27 revenue and margin guidance, OSAT and semiconductor-equipment monetisation timelines, and JV ramp-up schedules are all management or analyst views, not delivered results.
