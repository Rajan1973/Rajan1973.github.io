How the score works

Tiers

Tier 1 · Ranks 1–12 · Core buys. Strong quarter, delivered on guidance, clean balance sheet, and Q3 visibility. Size these as full positions.
Tier 2 · Ranks 13–25 · Accumulate. Good quarter with one open question, or thinner evidence. Build positions in steps.
Tier 3 · Ranks 26–47 · Selective. Best in their own sector, but weaker on price setup, leverage or data. Buy only on the trigger named in "What to track".

What FII/DII flows changed

The 47 names stay the same. Flows changed the order, not the selection: no stock on the list shows institutions leaving on a scale that outweighs its quarter, and only ITC Hotels comes close. Six names change tier.

  • Up into Tier 1: UltraTech (15 → 9; combined FII + DII +1.1 pts YoY and +1.5 QoQ), Exide (17 → 10; +1.4 YoY, FII +2.6 QoQ) and PN Gadgil (18 → 11; +5.0 YoY and +5.6 QoQ, the strongest fresh buying on the list).
  • Down to Tier 2: Transrail (8 → 15; −1.8 YoY, −1.2 QoQ), Techno Electric (11 → 16; −2.4 YoY) and ITC Hotels (10 → 18; FII −13.9 pts YoY). The ITC Hotels move is too large to be routine selling; a single large holder exiting is the likely cause, but that was not verified.
  • Biggest risers: Navin Fluorine (9 → 2; +5.2 YoY, DII +4.0) and Ujjivan (21 → 19; +16.9 YoY, mostly DII from the preferential issue). ACME Solar (+11.9 pts combined YoY), Mphasis (+9.5), NHPC (+6.8), Astra Microwave (+6.0), Engineers India (+4.0) and JSW Energy (+3.9) also moved up, mostly on domestic buying.
  • Biggest fallers outside the tier moves: CG Power (29 → 33; −2.0 YoY, −1.2 QoQ), IIFL (32 → 39; FII −2.7, DII −1.3), Fine Organic (33 → 40) and Ganesha Ecosphere (45 → 47; DII −6.4 YoY).
  • Hand-overs, not exits: at Max Healthcare (FII −11.3, DII +11.0), Vijaya (FII −7.6, DII +7.2), IHCL and Elgi, foreign funds sold to domestic funds. The combined holding barely moved, so these score neutral. Read them as a change of owner, not a verdict on the business.
  • The pattern: domestic funds were the net buyers across the list. FII holding fell YoY in 21 of the 45 names with comparable data; DII holding rose in 29.
Key takeaways · Conviction list

What the list says as a whole

  • Healthcare holds three of the top seven places: Medanta (1), Vijaya (6) and Dr Lal (7). Service businesses with net cash and pricing power came through a cost-shock quarter best.
  • The cheapest-looking strong prints are Timken, ITC Hotels, Transrail, Techno Electric, Max and Supreme. Each is flat to down over a year despite a clean quarter.
  • Already priced: Navin (+66%), Indo Count (+77%), Avalon (+95%), Sona (+89%) and Astra (+71%). Their quarters were excellent; their setups are not.
  • No small finance bank or vehicle financier makes Tier 1. The rate cycle has turned against funding costs; Ujjivan (19) and IIFL (39) are the only lenders on the list.
  • Healthcare (Medanta, Vijaya, Dr Lal) is the largest theme in the top 12. After the flow update, power transmission keeps only GE Vernova T&D there; Transrail and Techno move to Tier 2. Cap any one theme at about a quarter of the portfolio.

Key takeaways · Avoid list

What the avoid list says

  • Other income is the most common tell. Seven of the 25 lean on it: Afcons (3.3x PBT), Cochin Shipyard (more than PBT), BDL (68%), Mazagon Dock (63%), Raymond Lifestyle (54%), PI (34%) and SKF Industrial, whose PBT growth came from it. NLC's profit is a tax credit.
  • Defence PSUs supply three names. Mazagon Dock and BDL lost 10–16 pts of margin YoY, and Cochin Shipyard lost 13.6 pts QoQ. Down 32–36% in a year, they are still not cheap on core earnings.
  • Growth funded by the balance sheet is the second pattern: Kaynes, Amber, RR Kabel, Thangamayil and Sky Gold. All five come from the digest-based reviews (EMS, Cables, Jewellery), so re-verify before acting on them.
  • Contractors with stuck execution: KEC and Afcons have record order books that are not converting, and PNC is barred from its main client for three years.
  • Broken guidance is the sharpest single signal. KEC, PI, Amara Raja, Kaynes, Sky Gold and SAIL each missed or cut a commitment they had made earlier.
  • Institutions confirm the worst names. Kaynes (combined FII + DII −15.8 pts YoY), KEC (−9.0), PNC (−5.1), Raymond Lifestyle (−4.9), Apollo Pipes (−6.8) and Afcons (−3.6) are being sold; each held or moved up its place on the avoid list. Utkarsh, Sky Gold, Cochin Shipyard and HCG saw buying, which eased their risk scores; they stay on the list on fundamentals.
  • Not ranked for lack of data: Ultracab, Utssav CZ, Vedanta Oil & Gas and Jyoti Structures. No Q2 figures were found, which is itself a reason not to own them.

Sources and limits

  • Quarterly figures: Tijori's filed consolidated quarterlies, as used in each sector review (operating profit before other income). Company-reported figures are labelled where used.
  • Management commentary: call transcripts where the review read them (for example Navin, Vinati, PI and Mahindra Finance), and results coverage and dated broker notes otherwise. Broker targets show the report date as given in each review.
  • Avoid-list risk weights (after the flow update): earnings damage 22.5%, broken promises 18%, balance-sheet stress 18%, Q3 headwinds 18%, earnings-quality flags 13.5%, FII + DII selling 10%. Data confidence is shown but not scored.
  • FII and DII holdings: Tijori screener, September 2026 quarter against June 2026 (QoQ) and September 2025 (YoY), pulled 17 Nov 2026. Flow score: combined change of +3 pts or more YoY scores 5, +1 to +3 scores 4, −1 to +1 scores 3, −3 to −1 scores 2, below −3 scores 1. It drops a point if the latest quarter fell more than 1 pt, and rises one if it gained more than 1 pt on a rising year. Moves above 5 pts in one bucket can be block deals or reclassification. Groww (listed Nov 2025) uses QoQ only; Vedanta Aluminium and Antony Waste had no data and score neutral.
  • Factor scores (1–5) are analyst judgements made from each review's company card, KPI table and walk-the-talk table. They are not model outputs, and reasonable people could score some names a point differently.
  • This is research for information, not investment advice. Check the latest price, valuation and any news since 13 Nov before acting.
